📅 Last updated: September 25, 2026 · 08:00 AM CT · Week 17 edition

A 377-yard rushing avalanche in Week 8 didn't just beat Winnipeg — it rearranged the entire board, turning a 2-5 B.C. team into the league's loudest momentum story and knocking the Bombers down another peg. Montreal keeps lapping the field at 6-1 with a +62 differential and a CFL-low 19.4 points allowed per game, while the middle of the rankings is a pileup of winning records that don't all deserve equal trust. Week 17 is where the résumés get audited.

1 Montreal Alouettes 6-1 ◆ —

The only team in the league with no argument against it — 6-1, a league-best +62 point differential, and a defense surrendering just 19.4 points per game. That's not a hot start, that's a structural advantage. Until someone solves the defense, the gap between first and second is wider than the gap between second and sixth.

2 BC Lions 2-5 ▲ +4

A 2-5 record has never looked less like a 2-5 record. Rookie quarterback Kaidon Salter just piloted a 377-yard rushing performance that set a Winnipeg franchise record for futility, and the Lions have gone from afterthought to the league's biggest momentum story in one afternoon. The climb of four spots is a bet on trajectory, not résumé — and right now the trajectory is steep.

3 Saskatchewan Roughriders 5-2 ▼ -1

At 5-2 the Roughriders own the record, but a -11 point differential says the scoreboard has been kinder than the film. Winning ugly is a skill, and they've clearly got it, but a minus differential at this record is the kind of thing that regresses without warning. They drop a spot not because they lost, but because B.C. forced the issue.

4 Toronto Argonauts 4-3 ◆ —

The defending champs sit at 4-3 with the league's top passing attack at 310.6 yards per game and a +28 differential — the profile of a team that's better than its record. The only thing keeping them out of the top three is that everyone above them has either a better record or a hotter hand. That's a temporary problem.

5 Calgary Stampeders 5-2 ▼ -2

Same 5-2 record as Saskatchewan, two spots lower, and that's the entire point — the standings can't separate these teams, so the ranking has to. Calgary slides two spots in a week where it didn't lose ground on paper, which tells you how thin the margin is in the upper middle. This is a holding pattern, not a verdict.

6 Winnipeg Blue Bombers 4-4 ▼ -1

The 4-4 record undersells how bad Week 8 was — 377 rushing yards surrendered is a franchise record for embarrassment, and it happened with All-CFL right tackle Jarell Broxton already shelved by a knee injury, forcing Vanterpool to slide out to right tackle. Add a banged-up Zach Collaros, Jefferson's F-bomb, and Ceresna's "horrendous" verdict, and you have a locker room leaking from multiple seams. Dru Brown stepping in at quarterback is the least of their problems.

7 Edmonton Elks 3-4 ◆ —

At 3-4, the Elks are the best of the bottom tier and the only sub-.500 team here that isn't actively trending the wrong way. They hold station because nobody beneath them has earned the promotion. The gap to sixth is real, but so is the gap to eighth.

8 Ottawa Redblacks 3-5 ◆ —

A 3-5 record that includes a 36-34 loss to Winnipeg in Week 7 — a two-point margin against a team ranked two spots higher. That's the frustrating part of Ottawa's season: the losses are close enough to suggest the record is a rounding error. Close doesn't cash, though, and the Redblacks stay put.

9 Hamilton Tiger-Cats 2-6 ◆ —

At 2-6, the Tiger-Cats are the only team in the league with a record this far underwater, and there's no differential or momentum story to soften it. They hold at nine by default — nobody below them exists to take the spot. The climb starts with stopping the bleeding, not with style points.
