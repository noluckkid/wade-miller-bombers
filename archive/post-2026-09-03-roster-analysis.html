The Bombers' QB Gambit: Trading Depth for a Future Under Center

September 03, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Pennington Play: A Low-Cost Futures Bet

On Wednesday, the Winnipeg Blue Bombers executed a transaction that barely registered on the CFL's transaction wire but speaks volumes about their long-term planning. The club acquired the negotiation-list rights to Northern Arizona quarterback Ty Pennington from the Ottawa Redblacks, surrendering American linebacker Kyahva Tezino in the process. On its face, this is a minor deal — a swap of unproven assets. But context matters. Zach Collaros turns 36 in October and is playing on a contract that carries a significant cap hit. The Bombers have been content to ride Collaros as their starter, but they've also been quietly stockpiling quarterback assets. Pennington is the latest addition to that pipeline.

The six-foot-two, 220-pound passer has completed 65.0 percent of his passes for 5,568 yards, 34 touchdowns, and seven interceptions over 25 collegiate games at Northern Arizona. He was named the Big Sky Newcomer of the Year in 2024 and earned All-Big Sky honourable mention in 2025. Those are FCS numbers, which means they need to be taken with a grain of salt, but the efficiency — a 65 percent completion rate with a 34:7 touchdown-to-interception ratio — suggests a quarterback who processes the game well and doesn't force throws. His rushing production (691 yards, nine scores) adds a mobility dimension that Collaros, at 36, no longer possesses. Pennington spent his first two collegiate seasons at Pittsburg State, an NCAA Division II program, where he completed 67.0 percent of his passes for 447 yards, six touchdowns, and one interception, adding 359 rushing yards and four scores. The Bombers are betting on a developmental arc that has shown steady improvement at every level.

The Cost: Tezino's Departure and the Depth Calculus

The price for Pennington's rights was Kyahva Tezino, a 26-year-old American linebacker who had been on Winnipeg's roster since 2023. Tezino appeared in 12 games for the Bombers last season, primarily on special teams, and was a depth piece at linebacker. He wasn't a starter, and his departure doesn't create a hole in the starting lineup. But it does thin out the special teams unit, which has been a hallmark of Mike O'Shea's teams. The Bombers are essentially trading a known special teams contributor for an unknown quarterback prospect. That's a bet on positional value — quarterbacks are the most valuable asset in the CFL, and the Bombers are betting that Pennington's upside exceeds Tezino's floor.

This is also a reflection of the Bombers' cap situation. Winnipeg has committed significant money to Collaros, offensive lineman Patrick Neufeld, and a core of veteran players who have been to four straight Grey Cups. The cap is tight, and the Bombers can't afford to pay premium salaries to backup quarterbacks. By acquiring Pennington's rights via the negotiation list, they're getting a potential developmental quarterback at a fraction of the cost of a veteran backup. If Pennington pans out, they have a cheap, homegrown option to eventually replace Collaros. If he doesn't, they've lost a special teams player who was replaceable. The negotiation list mechanism is a first-come, first-served system that allows teams to claim up to 45 players' rights without any commitment — a low-risk, high-reward avenue that Winnipeg is exploiting aggressively.

The Physicality Question: Neufeld's Edge and the Montreal Rivalry

The Pennington trade wasn't the only story out of Winnipeg this week. Friday's game against Montreal at Princess Auto Stadium was a physical, chippy affair that saw Neufeld flagged three times — once for piling on, twice for unnecessary roughness. Neufeld, a 12-year CFL veteran, said postgame that he was looking to play "on the edge" against a defence that "get(s) after people." Head coach Mike O'Shea offered full support, complimenting the physicality of his offensive line. But there's a fine line between playing on the edge and crossing it, and Neufeld's three penalties in a single game is a concern — especially with the October 3 rematch in Montreal looming.

Alouettes quarterback Davis Alexander confirmed the rivalry is real, telling reporters Tuesday that "the teams don't like each other, obviously." Alexander admitted he let his emotions get the best of him during the game and that he talked to officials "probably way more than (he) should have." The 27-year-old declined to expand on what frustrated him, though it appears to have been penalty-related. Both teams were penalized six times during the game, with Neufeld accounting for half of Winnipeg's flags. The October 3 rematch is now circled on both calendars. For the Bombers, the question is whether their physicality — a defining characteristic of this roster — becomes a liability. Neufeld is a key piece of the offensive line, and if he's taking three penalties a game, that's a drive-killer. The Bombers need his aggression, but they also need him to channel it productively. O'Shea's postgame endorsement of the physicality suggests the coaching staff is willing to accept the penalty risk in exchange for the intimidation factor.

The Bigger Picture: Win-Now vs. Build-For-2027

The Bombers are in a unique position. They've been to four consecutive Grey Cups, winning two. The core of that team — Collaros, Neufeld, Willie Jefferson, Adam Bighill — is aging. The window is still open, but it's closing. That's why the Pennington trade is so interesting. It's a move that doesn't help the 2025 team at all. Pennington is still in college. He won't be available until 2026 at the earliest. But it's a move that positions the Bombers for the post-Collaros era, whenever that arrives.

This is the mark of a well-run organization. The Bombers aren't just trying to win now; they're trying to build a sustainable pipeline.
