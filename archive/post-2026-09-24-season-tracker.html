Collaros to the Six-Game IL: Winnipeg's Season Now Rides on Dru Brown

September 24, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Six-Game Label That Isn't Six Games

Zach Collaros is on the six-game injured list. Read that as a death sentence and you've misread the document. Winnipeg updated its depth chart and roster Thursday morning, listing the 38-year-old quarterback with a head injury after a full week as a non-participant in practice. He has gone 5-4 as a starter this season, completing 70.6 percent of his passes for 2,204 yards, 11 touchdowns and six interceptions.

Here is what matters more than the designation: the six-game injured list is not a six-game sentence. Players can be activated early, and Mike O'Shea said plainly that Collaros playing again this season is a strong possibility. Winnipeg already ran this exact sequence once this year — Collaros went on the one-game list first, was shifted to the six-game list, and missed four games total. The Bombers are not announcing a season-ending injury. They are buying roster flexibility while a 38-year-old with a head injury heals on a timeline nobody outside the building knows.

For the record, the career: 181 regular-season games, 68.1 percent completion, 38,187 yards, 227 touchdowns, 123 interceptions, plus 274 carries for 1,506 yards and 11 scores. Three Grey Cups. Two Most Outstanding Player awards. None of it is a projection for Friday night, and treating it as one is how you lose money on this team.

Dru Brown Is Not the Drop-Off You Think

The line moved hard on the Collaros news, which tells you the market still prices Winnipeg as a quarterback-driven team. The underlying numbers say otherwise. The Bombers have generated the third-fewest passing yards per game in the CFL this season. This is not an aerial circus that just lost its ringmaster. It is a team that asks its quarterback to hand the ball off, take the checkdown, and not hand the game away.

Dru Brown has done that. The 29-year-old made four starts in place of Collaros this year, going 2-2, and one of those wins came against Toronto in Week 6 — a 339-yard passing performance that is better than all but one of Collaros's totals this season. Brown has completed 70.2 percent of his passes for 1,148 yards, five touchdowns and five interceptions in 2025. Over 77 career regular-season games, he sits at 68.7 percent, 8,980 yards, 51 touchdowns and 27 interceptions.

The five interceptions in four starts is the number that decides this game. Brown does not need to steal it. He needs to not lose it. Winnipeg's coaching staff clearly understands the assignment — moving Canadian quarterback Taylor Elgersma off the reserve list is a depth-chart hedge, not a signal that the job is open.

The Defence Is the Actual Story

Two weeks ago in the Banjo Bowl, Winnipeg's defence dismantled Saskatchewan. The return of Redha Kramdi sparked the unit, and the Bombers shut down a Riders attack that had been moving the ball on everyone. Normally you dismiss that as an outlier. You should not dismiss it here, because a sizeable veteran checking back into the lineup is a structural change, not statistical noise. Kramdi's presence alters what Winnipeg can call on the back end, and the ripple runs through the entire front seven.

That matters enormously against Toronto, because the Argonauts are not the defence that showed up last week. Their performance against Edmonton had more to do with the Elks having an off day than with any stand the Boatmen made. This is still a unit with the third-worst net defence and the second-worst passing defence in the CFL. Brown already hung 339 on them once. The blueprint exists.

Playoff Math and the Power-Ranking Problem

Winnipeg has won seven of the 13 games started by Collaros or Brown this season, and with the regular season winding down, every remaining snap is a seeding argument. The West is a scrum — the difference between hosting a playoff game and crossing two time zones in November is one or two results.

This is also why the Bombers are the hardest team in the CFL to rank. Most power rankings slot them in the middle of the pack, and that is roughly correct, but it hides the shape of the team. Winnipeg's defence travels. Winnipeg's offence does not. A ranking that averages those two things together produces a number that describes neither. The honest read: this is a top-four defence attached to a bottom-four passing offence, and the variance between those two units is the entire season.

The market repriced Winnipeg the instant Collaros hit the six-game list. That is a public-perception move, not a football move. The football move is recognizing that the drop from Collaros to Brown in this specific offence is smaller than the drop from a healthy Kramdi to an absent one.

Friday Night: Toronto at Winnipeg, 8:00 p.m. EDT

The Argonauts arrive at Princess Auto Stadium on Friday, September 25 with the worst possible matchup profile: a leaky pass defence against a quarterback who has already beaten them, backed by a Winnipeg defence coming off its best performance of the year.

Three keys. First, Brown protects the football — five interceptions in four starts is the only stat that can beat the Bombers on Friday. Second, Winnipeg runs the ball and controls tempo, keeping Toronto's offence off the field and its own defence fresh. Third, the front seven pressures the Argonauts into the kind of off-schedule throws that turned last week's Edmonton game into a mirage.

Winnipeg does not need Collaros on Friday. It needs the version of itself that showed up in the Banjo Bowl. If that team is real, the Bombers win this game and stay alive in the West. If it was a one-week spike, the six-game injured list just became the story of the season.
