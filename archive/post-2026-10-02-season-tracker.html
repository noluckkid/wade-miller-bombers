Bombers Get Whole Just in Time: Wilson and Griffin II Return for Montreal Test

October 02, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The First Complete Receiving Corps of 2025

For twelve weeks, the Winnipeg Blue Bombers have been running a passing game held together with tape and improvisation. That ends Saturday in Montreal. Ontaria Wilson returns from a hand injury that cost him two games, and for the first time this season the Bombers will line up Tim White, Nic Demski, Wilson and Dorian Singer together in the starting receiving corps.

The numbers explain why this matters. Wilson has 56 receptions for 739 yards and five touchdowns across 12 regular-season games, ranking third on the team behind White and Demski. That is a No. 3 receiver producing at a No. 2 rate — and he has been doing it while the group around him shuffled week to week. Over 36 career regular-season games, the 27-year-old native of Ashburn, Ga. has 148 receptions for 2,147 yards and 11 touchdowns. He is not a spare part. He is a chain-mover, and Winnipeg's offence has been missing exactly that on second down.

The corresponding roster move tells you how the coaching staff views the hierarchy: rookie Kenneth Womack, who dressed for Winnipeg's last two games without registering a statistic, has been demoted to the practice roster. That is not a knock on Womack. It is an admission that the Bombers' receiver room, when healthy, has no room for developmental reps in a playoff race.

Griffin II and the Ratio Math That Decides Games

Michael Griffin II's return at strong-side linebacker is the quieter but arguably more consequential activation. He was Winnipeg's day-one starter at the SAM spot and has missed six games with an ankle injury. In his abbreviated season he has 35 defensive tackles, one special teams tackle and one forced fumble. Across 49 career regular-season games with the Blue Bombers and Calgary Stampeders, he has posted 135 defensive tackles, 31 special teams tackles, one tackle for loss, four pass knockdowns, three sacks, one forced fumble, one fumble recovery and one touchdown.

Those are starter-calibre numbers, but the real value is structural. Griffin II is an American playing a position where Winnipeg has been forced to improvise. Canadian defender Jaylen Smith filled in for all six games Griffin II missed — and Smith has now been moved to the reserve roster after being listed as questionable with a hip injury, a non-participant in Thursday's practice.

Read that sequence again. The Bombers lost their American SAM, replaced him with a Canadian, and now the Canadian replacement is hurt in the same week the American returns. On the surface that is a wash. In practice it is a ratio reset: Winnipeg can deploy Griffin II at SAM without burning a national starting spot, which frees a Canadian body elsewhere on the depth chart. In a league where the ratio dictates which four plays you can call inside the twenty, that is worth more than any single tackle.

Week 18 and the West Division Squeeze

Context matters here. Week 18 is the point of the CFL calendar where the standings stop being abstract. Three of this week's four games carry playoff implications. The outlier is Friday's Hamilton-at-Ottawa matchup, a game between two eliminated teams that exists mostly so the Redblacks can try to avoid becoming the first team in CFL history to start a season 0-15.

Winnipeg's trip to Montreal is not that. It is a game with seeding consequences, and it arrives at the exact moment the Bombers' roster is as close to full strength as it has been since June. That is the tension baked into every power ranking that keeps Winnipeg in the top tier: the Bombers have been graded on their ceiling all year, not their floor. The ceiling is a receiving corps of White, Demski, Wilson and Singer with a healthy Griffin II behind them. The floor is what we watched for six weeks — a team winning on scheme and quarterback play because the bodies simply were not there.

Power rankings are a lagging indicator. They reward résumés. What they cannot capture is the difference between a team that has been surviving and a team that is finally whole. Winnipeg has spent the season in the second category while being ranked in the first. Saturday is the first real test of whether those two things converge.

Matchup Preview: Winnipeg at Montreal

The schematic question is simple: can Montreal's secondary hold up against four legitimate receiving threats? Most CFL defences can bracket one receiver and survive a second. Very few can account for White, Demski, Wilson and Singer simultaneously without leaving a linebacker isolated in coverage or sacrificing run support on first down.

That is where Griffin II's return compounds. A healthy SAM gives Winnipeg's defensive staff the flexibility to blitz or drop eight without tipping its hand — the kind of disguise that turns third-and-medium into a punt. The Bombers' defence has been asked to protect leads all year. Getting Griffin II back means it can also create them.

The risk is rust, and it is real. Wilson has not played in two weeks. Griffin II has not played in six. Hand injuries affect catch radius and contested-ball strength; ankle injuries affect the change-of-direction that makes a SAM effective in space. Expect both to be on snap counts, and expect Montreal to test Griffin II early with a slotback release into the flat.

The Verdict

Winnipeg's season has been defined by who was not available. That excuse expires Saturday. The Bombers are getting their No. 3 receiver and their starting SAM back in the same week, at the point in the calendar when roster health is worth more than any schematic adjustment. If they lose in Montreal with a full deck, the problem was never the training room.
