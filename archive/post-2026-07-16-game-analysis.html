He caught the ball at his own 11-yard line, then executed a textbook lateral cut behind a wall set by fullback Bailey Feltmate and linebacker Shayne Gauthier. The Dru Brown Conundrum: Play-Action Misdirection vs. Ottawa’s Blitz-Heavy Scheme

With Zach Collaros nursing a shoulder issue, Dru Brown is expected to start against the RedBlacks on July 13. Brown’s performance against Toronto—339 passing yards, one touchdown, zero interceptions—was his best as a Bomber, but the film reveals a specific schematic reliance. On 22 of his 34 dropbacks, offensive coordinator Buck Pierce used play-action fakes to running back Brady Oliveira. Brown completed 17 of those 22 passes for 268 yards, per Pro Football Focus data. The reason is clear: Ottawa’s defence, under coordinator Barron Miles, blitzes on 38.2% of snaps, third-highest in the league. Play-action forces linebackers to hesitate for a half-second, buying Brown time to hit crossing routes over the middle. The matchup to watch is Brown versus RedBlacks defensive back Brandin Dandridge. Dandridge has allowed a 72.3% completion rate when targeted this season, and Brown’s deep ball—he averages 9.8 air yards per attempt—will test that vulnerability. However, Winnipeg’s offensive line has allowed 11 sacks through five games, tied for fourth-most. If Ottawa’s blitz gets home early, Pierce may need to shift to quick screens and RPOs, which Brown ran effectively in his two seasons as Ottawa’s starter (2024-25). The emotional narrative is obvious, but the tactical reality is that Brown’s success hinges on whether Winnipeg’s protection can hold for 2.5 seconds or more.

Defensive Fractures: Why Winnipeg’s Secondary Is Vulnerable to Vertical Routes

Defensive coordinator Jordan Younger’s unit is allowing 28.4 points per game, eighth in the CFL, and the film shows a specific weakness: deep post routes. On 12 targets of 20+ air yards against Winnipeg, opposing quarterbacks have completed nine (75%) for 287 yards and three touchdowns. The culprit is alignment discipline. In Week 2 against Saskatchewan, Trevor Harris hit Kian Schaffer-Baker on a 54-yard post when cornerback Tyrell Ford bit on a shallow crosser, leaving safety Brandon Alexander isolated. Against Toronto on July 4, Chad Kelly connected with DaVaris Daniels on a 38-yard post when Alexander and halfback Demerio Houston failed to communicate on a switch. The Bombers are running a Cover-2 shell on 61% of snaps, per CFL.ca data, but their safeties are consistently late rotating to the deep middle. The solution may lie in more press-man coverage on the outside. Ford and Houston have combined for four pass breakups in man coverage, compared to just one in zone. If Younger dares to leave his corners on islands, it could disrupt timing routes—but it also risks giving up big plays if the pass rush, which has only seven sacks (tied for sixth), doesn’t get home. The RedBlacks, despite their 0-5 record, have a deep threat in receiver Jaelon Acklin, who averages 16.2 yards per catch. This is the chess match that will decide the game.
