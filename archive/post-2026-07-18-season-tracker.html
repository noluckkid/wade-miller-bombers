Dru Brown’s Revenge Tour: Can Winnipeg Survive Without Collaros?

July 18, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

Standings and the West Division Race

Through Week 6 of the 2025 CFL season, the Winnipeg Blue Bombers sit at 3-2, good for second place in the West Division. They trail the 4-1 Saskatchewan Roughriders by one game but hold a crucial head-to-head win from Week 2. The B.C. Lions (3-2) are tied with Winnipeg in the loss column, while the Calgary Stampeders (2-3) and Edmonton Elks (1-4) lag behind. The Bombers’ +27 point differential is second-best in the league, powered by a defence that has allowed just 21.4 points per game. However, their offence ranks fifth in yards per game (345.2), a number that will be tested without their starter.

Stats Leaders: Who’s Carrying the Load

With Collaros sidelined, the statistical burden shifts. Through five games, Brady Oliveira leads the CFL in rushing with 482 yards and three touchdowns, averaging 5.6 yards per carry. His 112-yard effort in Week 5 against Hamilton was the difference in a 14-13 slugfest. Receiver Nic Demski has emerged as the top target, hauling in 27 catches for 378 yards and two scores. On defence, linebacker Adam Bighill leads the team with 32 tackles, while defensive end Willie Jefferson has four sacks and a forced fumble. Dru Brown, in his lone start, completed 25 of 31 passes for 339 yards and a touchdown against Toronto—a performance that earned him CFL offensive player of the week honours.

Playoff Picture: The Collaros Cloud

The Bombers’ playoff odds remain strong, but the six-game injured list placement of Collaros (neck) changes the calculus. Winnipeg’s next three opponents—Ottawa (0-5), Edmonton (1-4), and Montreal (2-3)—have a combined 3-12 record. If Brown can replicate his Week 5 efficiency, the Bombers could be 6-2 by the time Collaros is eligible to return in Week 9. The West Division crossover is a real threat: the East’s fourth-place team (currently Montreal) has a worse record than Winnipeg. A 10-8 finish should secure a home playoff game, but anything less than 11-7 risks a road semifinal.

Matchup Preview: Dru Brown vs. Ottawa Redblacks

Sunday’s game in Ottawa is dripping with narrative. Brown was traded by the Redblacks on July 28 after they committed to Jake Maier as their starter. Ottawa is 0-5, with Maier posting a 60.1 completion percentage, five touchdowns, and seven interceptions. The Redblacks’ defence ranks seventh in points allowed (28.4 per game) and last in sacks (six). Brown knows their tendencies—he spent two seasons in their system. Expect Winnipeg to lean on Oliveira early, then use play-action to Demski and rookie receiver Dalton Schoen. The key matchup is Bombers offensive line vs. Ottawa’s front seven: if Winnipeg’s line, which has allowed 12 sacks, gives Brown time, he can exploit a secondary that has surrendered 10 passing touchdowns. The Redblacks’ only hope is to force turnovers—they have eight takeaways, tied for second-most in the league.

The Revenge Game Factor

Brown is publicly downplaying the revenge narrative, but the numbers don’t lie. In his first start against his former team, he posted a 116.2 passer rating. The Bombers’ offence averaged 7.2 yards per play in that game, compared to 5.4 in their other four contests. Ottawa’s defence, meanwhile, has allowed 30-plus points in three of five games. If Brown can replicate that efficiency, Winnipeg will cover the 6.5-point spread. But if the Redblacks’ pass rush—led by defensive end Lorenzo Mauldin IV (three sacks)—gets home, the Bombers could be in a dogfight. The X-factor is weather: forecasts call for rain and 20 km/h winds, which could neutralize the deep ball and force both teams to run. That favours Oliveira and Winnipeg’s ground game.
