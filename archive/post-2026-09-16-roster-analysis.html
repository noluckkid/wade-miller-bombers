Bombers' Roster Roulette: Winnipeg's Transaction Wire Tells a Tale of Desperation

September 16, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Collaros Cap Crunch

Zach Collaros turns 38 in March, and the Blue Bombers' front office has spent the last six weeks pretending that isn't a problem. The veteran quarterback is playing on a restructured deal that pushed $412,000 of his 2026 salary into signing-bonus proration — a move that bought cap relief this spring but created a $287,000 dead-money anchor if Winnipeg dares to move on. That restructuring was the first domino in a chain of desperate transactions that has defined the Bombers' September.

The backup situation tells the real story. Winnipeg signed veteran pivot Jake Dolegala to the practice squad on September 8 after he was released by the Toronto Argonauts, then immediately elevated him to the active roster when Collaros missed two practices with a throwing-shoulder issue in Week 14. Dolegala has thrown exactly 11 CFL passes since 2023. The Bombers also kicked the tires on former Edmonton backup Tre Ford in mid-September, but Ford's $185,000 asking price — plus the compensation Edmonton demanded in a trade — sent Winnipeg scrambling to the waiver wire instead. They claimed quarterback Ben Holmes off the Ottawa Redblacks' practice roster on September 15, a move that screams "we have no plan" louder than any press release.

The Defensive Rebuild on the Fly

Adam Bighill's one-year, $165,000 deal signed in February was supposed to be a farewell tour. Instead, it's become a liability. The 37-year-old linebacker has missed three games this season with a hamstring injury, and in the two games he's played since returning, he's registered just four tackles and zero sacks. Winnipeg's response on the transaction wire has been panic: they signed former Calgary Stampeders linebacker Micah Awe to a two-year deal on September 12, a contract worth $140,000 per season with a $50,000 signing bonus. Awe is 31 and was cut by Calgary in August precisely because his coverage skills had eroded.

The secondary has been an even bigger revolving door. Starting cornerback Jamal Peters went down with a torn pec in Week 13, and the Bombers have cycled through four different replacements since. They signed 26-year-old American defensive back Tre'Vaughn Mincy off the B.C. Lions' practice roster on September 10, then cut him six days later after he allowed a 74-yard touchdown against Saskatchewan — the same game where Roughriders DB Seyi Oladipo Jr. picked off Collaros twice. Winnipeg's response was to sign 30-year-old CFL journeyman Brandon Dozier on September 18, a player who hadn't taken a regular-season snap since 2024. Dozier was immediately inserted into the starting lineup for Week 16, a decision that reeks of a coaching staff throwing darts at a board.

Receiver Roulette and the National Ratio

The Bombers' receiving corps has been a churn of imports and national-ratio casualties. Winnipeg cut veteran slotback Greg Ellingson on September 5 — a move that saved $92,000 against the cap but forced them to start rookie Canadian Kian Schaffer-Baker in his place. Schaffer-Baker has 14 catches for 168 yards in four starts, numbers that would be fine for a first-year player but are a downgrade from Ellingson's 38 catches and 412 yards before his release. The transaction wire shows the Bombers signed three different American receivers between September 7 and September 16: Tyrell Shavers (waived by the Elks), DaeSean Hamilton (out of football since 2023), and Justin Hall (cut by the Tiger-Cats in August). None of them have made a catch in a Winnipeg uniform.

The national-ratio math is brutal. Winnipeg currently starts four Canadians on offence and three on defence, leaving them one short of the CFL's minimum in certain alignments. That forced them to dress rookie offensive lineman Liam Dobson — a 2025 draft pick who was a healthy scratch for the first nine weeks — as a sixth offensive lineman in Week 15 just to satisfy the ratio. Dobson was subsequently placed on the practice squad on September 19, replaced by veteran national lineman Chris Kolankowski, who was signed off the street after being released by the Roughriders in June. Kolankowski is 34 and hasn't played a snap since 2025. This is not a championship-calibre approach; it's a patchwork.

The Practice Squad as a Lifeboat

Winnipeg's practice roster has become a graveyard of failed experiments. As of September 20, the Bombers' practice squad includes four players who were on the active roster at some point this season: Dolegala, Mincy, Shavers, and defensive lineman TyJuan Garbutt, who was cut on September 14 after recording just one sack in seven games. The churn is costing Winnipeg real money — each practice-squad transaction carries a $2,500 administrative fee, and the Bombers have made 23 such moves since Labour Day. That's $57,500 in fees alone, money that could have been spent on actual talent.

The deeper issue is that Winnipeg's front office is operating without a coherent long-term vision. They've committed $1.2 million in 2027 cap space to players currently on the injured list — Collaros, Bighill, Peters, and defensive end Willie Jefferson, who has been playing through a knee injury that has limited him to 3 sacks in 12 games. The Bombers are 7-6 and sitting in a three-way tie for the final playoff spot, but their transaction wire reads like a team that knows it's running out of time. Every signing is a short-term gamble; every cut is an admission that the previous gamble failed. With five games left and the B.C. Lions holding the season-series tiebreaker after a 16-point win in Week 10, Winnipeg's margin for error is gone. The roster moves aren't building toward anything — they're just delaying the inevitable reckoning.

The Bombers have five games left, and their schedule is brutal: two against the Elks, one against the Lions, one against the Roughriders, and one against the Stampeders.
