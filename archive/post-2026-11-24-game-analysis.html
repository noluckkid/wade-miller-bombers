Roberts Isn't a Tackle Machine. He's Winnipeg's A-Gap Patch

November 24, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Number That Lies

Ninety-three defensive tackles in 18 games. That's the line BlueBombers.com led with on Nov. 23, and it's the line every fan will repeat between now and training camp. It's also the least interesting thing about Jacob Roberts. Tackle totals in the CFL are a function of three variables, snap count, scheme, and statistician generosity, and Roberts checked all three boxes in Calgary last season. He played 18 games. He played for a Stampeders defense that spent long stretches on the field. And he played the WILL spot, the position designed to clean up what the front seven spills.

Strip the volume down and the profile sharpens. Roberts averaged 5.2 defensive tackles per game, added nine more on special teams, and registered four sacks and one forced fumble. That last number matters: one forced fumble in 18 games says he's a catch-and-drag tackler, not a takeaway generator. At 6-1, 233 pounds out of Wake Forest and North Carolina A&T, he is a downhill thumper who runs to the ball, and the Bombers signed him to a one-year deal on Nov. 23 knowing exactly what that is, and what it isn't.

The Fit in Jordan Younger's Front

Here's the schematic problem Winnipeg has been papering over since the backend of the 2024 season. Jordan Younger's defense is built on a two-high shell and second-level force players who can scrape over the top of a down block and still make the tackle at the point of attack. When the second level gets reached, when the Mike gets sealed by a climbing guard and the Will gets kicked out by a fullback or an H-back, the whole structure leaks. Inside zone and power became the Bombers' kryptonite, and it wasn't because the defensive line got blown up. It's because the linebackers were getting tied up a half-step late.

That's the rep Roberts wins. His Calgary tape is full of him taking on the climb from a guard, stacking the block, and letting the safety run the alley clean. He isn't a sideline-to-sideline player in the Bighill mold; he's a between-the-tackles player who plays with his hands and his feet in sync. In a division where the run game is designed to stress the Mike, that's a tool, not a luxury.

The Blitz Rep and the A-Gap Mug

The four sacks are the tell on his pass-rush usage. Calgary didn't ask him to win the edge, they asked him to time the A-gap. Watch the mug looks: Roberts walked up over the center, showed blitz, bailed into a shallow zone, then came on a delayed call when the protection slid. That's a CFL-specific skill set, because the A-gap mug forces the center to declare and the quarterback to reset the protection. If Winnipeg's defensive staff wants to manufacture pressure without sending a boundary corner, and Younger has been reluctant to do that. Roberts is the body that makes the mug look real.

The flip side: 4.6 speed, not 4.4. He isn't chasing a waggle motion receiver across the field and he isn't covering a slot on a crosser in man. CFL offenses will find that out by Week 3 and start flexing backs and slots to his side.

The Coverage Question Nobody Wants to Ask

Roberts had one pass knockdown and one interception in three Vikings preseason games, and the interception came on a tipped ball in the Denver game on Aug. 28, the same game he hyperextended his knee in the third quarter and lost his NFL shot. In Calgary, the coverage production was thin. No interceptions listed, limited pass knockdowns. For a CFL linebacker, that's a red flag, because the position is asked to carry the seam, wall the flat, and handle the waggle in a league that runs more horizontal stress than any other.

The one-year contract is the front office saying the quiet part out loud: they want to see whether his coverage can survive a CFL season before they commit to a second one. He also had a third-round UFL Draft selection from Orlando, which is leverage he didn't use, a sign he wants to be here, but also a sign this is a prove-it deal in both directions.

The Hidden Yards: Sheahan and the Special Teams Math

Buried in the same news cycle was the Jamieson Sheahan re-signing, and it matters more than the aggregate punt average. Sheahan's value is directional, pinning returners to the boundary and forcing fair catches, and a defense that plays a two-high shell needs to win field position to keep that shell honest. Roberts' nine special-teams tackles in 2025 point to a player who will contribute on the coverage units, which is exactly how a one-year linebacker earns a second contract in this league.

The real test comes in Week 1, first-and-10, when an offense runs power at the Mike and sees whether the new guy can hold the point. The 93 tackles will be forgotten by then. The gap he fills, or doesn't, won't be.
