Bombers' Roster Roulette: How Transaction Missteps Sank Winnipeg to 4-4

August 03, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Cost of Loyalty: Re-Signing the Core

When the CFL transaction wire lit up on January 16, 2025, the Winnipeg Blue Bombers were praised for keeping their championship core intact. Running back Brady Oliveira signed a two-year extension worth $420,000 annually — the richest deal for a Canadian back in league history. Wide receiver Nic Demski followed with a one-year, $280,000 pact, and defensive end Willie Jefferson restructured his deal to clear cap room. On paper, this looked like a front office preserving a dynasty. On the field, it has become an anchor.

By the time the Bombers limped into their second bye week on July 28, those three contracts combined consumed over 22% of the team's salary cap. The consequence was predictable: the offensive line — the engine behind two Grey Cup titles — was left to rot. Only two starters from the 2024 unit returned, and the team's most significant transaction up front was signing undrafted free agent Brody Sutter off the University of Saskatchewan's roster. Sutter made his first career start on July 24 against B.C. and surrendered three sacks before being pulled. The Bombers' front office chose to pay for past glory rather than future protection, and the ledger now shows a team that ranks seventh in net offence and last in rushing defense.

The Injury Ledger and the Practice Squad Carousel

Injuries have decimated Winnipeg, but the transactions they triggered have made matters worse. As of July 22, the Bombers had four starters on the six-game injured list, including right tackle Anthony Miller (knee) and middle linebacker Jake Thomas (shoulder). The CFL transactions wire for that week shows a frantic series of moves: defensive end Davon Coleman was promoted from the practice roster on July 20, then released two days later when Thomas was placed back on the roster. Cornerback Marquise Bridges was signed off the Edmonton Elks' practice squad on July 21 and immediately plugged into the starting unit — he was in coverage on both of Zander Horvath's 58- and 35-yard touchdown runs in the fourth quarter of the 35-19 loss to B.C.

The math is brutal. Winnipeg allowed a franchise-record 377 rushing yards to the Lions, breaking a mark that stood since 1956. The Bombers' average of 5.9 yards per carry allowed through eight games is the worst in the CFL, and the transactions reflect a team reacting instead of anticipating. When veteran defensive tackle Jamal Woods went down with an Achilles tear on July 14, the team waited nine days before signing former Toronto Argonaut lineman Chris Kolankowski to the practice squad. Kolankowski was inactive for the B.C. game, leaving the interior of the defensive line with two natural defensive tackles — one of whom had never played a CFL snap. This is not a depth problem; this is a roster-management failure with a paper trail.

The Four-Quarter Failure: No Deadline Additions

Two consecutive home losses by 22 and 16 points have exposed a front office that refused to engage in the transaction market. at the July 16 CFL trade deadline, the Bombers neither swung a deal for a pass rusher nor poached a proven offensive lineman from another club's surplus. General manager Kyle Walters told reporters on July 25 that "we like our room," but the data contradicts him. Winnipeg's defence has allowed 11 rushing touchdowns, tied for most in the league, and their 20 quarterback sacks put them in the bottom third. The team's only acquisition in the week before the deadline was defensive back Cole Bishop, a practice-roster designate from Calgary who was cut on July 24 after failing his physical.

The 2025 Bombers are a study in contrast with the 2021 models that won the West Division with a rebuilt offensive line and a defence that finished first in rushing yards allowed. Those clubs made aggressive mid-season moves — acquiring offensive lineman Stanley Jones in 2021 and defensive end Garret Cleveland in 2022. This year's transaction wire is silent. The team's leading rusher, Oliveira, has 678 yards on the season, but the defence's inability to stop the run has put him on the sidelines in the second half — the Bombers have been outscored 67-24 in the fourth quarter this season. No transaction can fix a broken cap structure, but the refusal to make any moves at all has turned a playoff contender into a .500 team watching its season slip away.

Winnipeg's next game is September 5 against the Saskatchewan Roughriders, but the front office's work is already done — and it's not enough. The Bombers have 12 players on the six-game injured list, a practice squad of developmental unknowns, and a salary cap with less than $60,000 of flexibility. The roster choices made in January and February, and the inaction in July, are the real reason this team is 4-4. The players know it. The transaction wire proves it.

What the Bombers Must Do Now

The bye week offers a desperate opportunity to correct course. The most logical move is to sign veteran defensive tackle Ellis Thomas, who remains a free agent after being released by Hamilton in June. Thomas would cost $120,000 against the cap, but the Bombers have no room unless they rework Oliveira's deal or release pending free agent defensive back Dante Hall. The alternative is to watch the run defence continue to be carved up. Winnipeg is on pace to surrender 2,500 rushing yards — the second-worst mark in CFL history. The transaction decisions over the next two weeks will define whether this season ends in a Grey Cup appearance or a .500 slog. History, and the salary cap, say the latter.
