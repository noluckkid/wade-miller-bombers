Bombers' QB Carousel Exposes Roster Depth as Collaros Sits Again

The Collaros Injury and the Brown Gamble

Zach Collaros did not practice on Friday and will miss his second straight game Sunday night in Ottawa, head coach Mike O'Shea confirmed. The reigning CFL Most Outstanding Player suffered a neck injury in Winnipeg's July 5 loss to the Hamilton Tiger-Cats—a game where rookie Taylor Elgersma came off the bench to finish under centre. That forced the Blue Bombers to turn to Dru Brown, who responded with a 25-of-31 performance (81 percent completion rate) for 339 yards, one touchdown, and one interception in last week's win over the Toronto Argonauts. Brown was acquired via trade from Ottawa just 24 days ago, making his return to TD Place a storyline O'Shea downplayed but cannot escape. "His emotional intelligence is extremely high," O'Shea said Friday. "I don't ever really worry about highs and lows with that guy." Yet the Bombers' quarterback depth chart now reads: Brown (starter), Elgersma (backup), and Collaros (injured). That is one trade acquisition, one rookie, and one veteran with a neck issue—a thin margin for a team with Grey Cup aspirations.

Special Teams as the Roster Safety Net

While the quarterback carousel spins, Winnipeg's special teams unit has emerged as the roster's most consistent pillar through five games. Coordinator Mike Miller's group ranks first or second in multiple CFL statistical categories, anchored by Trey Vaval's franchise-record 129-yard missed field goal return for a touchdown against Toronto. Vaval is tied for second in the league with six "big plays"—three on punts, two on kickoffs, and that one missed field goal score. "We're basically the keepers of momentum," Miller said. The unit's production has masked offensive inconsistency: the Bombers have scored 20 or fewer points in two of five games, including a 16-14 loss to Hamilton. With Collaros out, the special teams' ability to flip field position and generate explosive plays becomes even more critical. Miller's group has created three touchdowns through five weeks, a pace that would rank among the league's best over a full season.

Roster Transactions and the 24-Day Trade Window

The Brown trade itself—executed on June 13, 2024—was a calculated roster move that now looks prescient. Winnipeg sent an undisclosed draft pick to Ottawa for a quarterback who had thrown only 47 career passes in two CFL seasons. The transaction wire shows the Bombers acted quickly after Collaros's injury scare in training camp, but the timing also reflects a broader roster philosophy: stockpile veteran arms even if it means carrying three quarterbacks on the active roster. Brown's 81 percent completion rate against Toronto suggests the gamble is paying off, but the sample size remains tiny. One game does not erase the fact that Winnipeg's offence has averaged just 23.4 points per game—down from 28.1 in 2023. The Bombers have also made three practice roster moves since July 1, adding offensive lineman Liam Dobson and defensive back Jamal Parker while releasing receiver Carlton Agudosi. Those transactions signal a front office still searching for the right mix around a banged-up quarterback room.

The Cap Implications of Quarterback Depth

Carrying three quarterbacks—Collaros at a reported $525,000, Brown at $185,000, and Elgersma at $80,000—eats roughly 12 percent of Winnipeg's salary cap. That is manageable for a contender, but only if Collaros returns healthy. If his neck injury lingers, the Bombers face a difficult choice: keep three QBs and sacrifice depth elsewhere, or release one and hope Brown stays healthy. The CFL's transaction wire shows no corresponding cuts to offset the Brown acquisition, meaning Winnipeg has absorbed his salary without shedding another contract. That is a bet on short-term success, not long-term roster flexibility. The Bombers are 3-2, but their next three games are against Ottawa, Calgary, and Saskatchewan—all teams with winning records. If Brown falters, the front office may need to scan the waiver wire for another arm, a move that would further strain the cap.

Injury Report and the Practice Squad Shuffle

Beyond Collaros, the Bombers' injury report lists defensive end Willie Jefferson (knee) and receiver Nic Demski (hamstring) as questionable for Sunday. Both are key contributors: Jefferson leads the team with three sacks, while Demski has 18 catches for 247 yards.
