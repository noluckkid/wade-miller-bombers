Collaros Is Back, But Winnipeg's Season Hinges on the A-Gap

October 10, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Centre Swap Is a Protection Decision, Not a Depth-Chart Formality

Tyler Elsbury's afternoon against Montreal was a three-act disaster: a holding penalty, a bad snap, and a sack allowed. In a vacuum, that's one bad game. Inside Winnipeg's offence, it's a systemic warning.

The Bombers run a shotgun-heavy, quick-game passing attack built on rhythm throws and play-action off inside zone. That architecture puts the centre at the fulcrum. He makes the protection call, he owns the A-gap, and he delivers a snap that has to arrive at Zach Collaros's chest every single rep. When the snap is off, the timing of a three-step drop is gone before it starts — and Collaros, at 38, is not the quarterback who's going to escape a free rusher and improvise.

Tui Eli isn't a talent upgrade so much as a certainty upgrade. The 30-year-old has 85 career regular-season games with Winnipeg at centre and guard since being taken in the fourth round of the 2019 CFL Draft out of Hawaii. He made one start at centre earlier this season when Elsbury slid to right guard in place of Patrick Neufeld. That's thin 2025 tape, but it's enough for the staff to know what they're getting: a veteran who will identify the Mike correctly and get the ball back to the quarterback.

Against Calgary, that matters more than usual. The Stampeders generate pressure with simulated looks and late A-gap walk-ups, forcing the centre to sort out who's actually rushing and who's dropping. Elsbury's misidentification last week produced the sack. Eli's job Saturday is boring and essential: get the front right, snap it clean, and let Collaros operate on schedule.

Collaros's Return Resets the Offence — and Its Ceiling

Collaros is 5-4 as a starter this season, completing 70.6 percent of his passes for 2,204 yards, 11 touchdowns, and six interceptions. He has missed six regular-season games. He is 38 years old.

Read those numbers together and you get the profile of a quarterback who is accurate, efficient, and no longer asked to win games with his arm alone. Eleven touchdowns in nine starts is a modest rate — 1.22 per game — and it reflects an offence that has deliberately shifted its scoring weight onto Brady Oliveira and the run game, particularly in the red zone.

That's the trade Winnipeg has made. The 70.6 percent completion rate is a function of a route tree that lives between the numbers: quick outs, sit-down routes against zone, and play-action crossers off run action. It's a low-variance, high-efficiency design. The problem is that low-variance offences stall when the field compresses, and stalled drives mean Sergio Castillo field goals instead of touchdowns.

The two-game absence matters here. Dru Brown and Bryce Perkins are both more mobile than Collaros, and there's a real chance the offence drifted toward movement-based concepts while he was out. Reinstalling Collaros means reinstalling the timing-based version — and that version only functions if the protection holds. Which brings us back to the A-gap, and to why the centre change and the quarterback change are really the same decision.

Jaylen Smith at SAM Is the Matchup Lever Against Calgary's Slot Game

The third change is the most interesting schematically. Jaylen Smith returns from a hip injury to start at strong-side linebacker — his seventh start of the year — with 43 total tackles. Michael Griffin II, who filled in last week and has 38 defensive tackles, moves to a depth role behind him.

In Jordan Younger's defence, the SAM is not a linebacker in the traditional sense. He's a hybrid: a run-support edge setter who also has to carry the slotback in Cover 2 and match him in man under. Against Calgary, that's the whole ballgame. The Stampeders' offence is built to attack the seam and the intermediate window between the hook defender and the safety, and the SAM is the man who has to take that space away.

Smith's 43 tackles in roughly ten games is a healthy rate for the position, and it tells you he's been around the ball in run support. The question is coverage. A hip injury is precisely the kind of ailment that erodes a defender's ability to flip his hips and transition — the exact skill the SAM needs against a slot receiver running an out-and-up.

If Smith is healthy, Winnipeg can stay in its base 4-3 and match personnel without compromise. If he isn't, Younger faces a bad choice: expose Smith in coverage, or go to a dime look that thins the run defence against a Calgary offence that will happily hand the ball off all afternoon if you let it.

The Play-Calling Tightrope: Red-Zone Math Meets Calgary's Pressure Package

Watch the first-and-10 calls Saturday. Winnipeg's offence under Buck Pierce has always been a run-first, play-action-second operation, and the Bombers lean on 21-personnel with Oliveira and a fullback to create downhill angles. That's a fine way to move the ball between the twenties.

Inside the twenty, the field compresses and the play-action window closes. That's where Collaros's modest touchdown rate becomes a structural issue rather than a statistical footnote. Winnipeg's historical answer has been to lean harder on Oliveira — but Calgary's front is built to stop exactly that, and the Stampeders will sell out to make Collaros beat them on second and long.

The counter is play-action on early downs in the red zone, and it requires the run game to stay credible for three quarters. If Winnipeg falls behind and becomes one-dimensional, the protection problems that cost Elsbury his job will resurface, and the A-gap will be the first place Calgary attacks — with a fresh centre making his second start of the season and a 38-year-old quarterback who can't outrun the mistake.

This is a game Winnipeg should win on paper. But the three lineup changes aren't cosmetic.
