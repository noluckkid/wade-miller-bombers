Bombers' Roster Rebuild: How Winnipeg's Transaction Gambles Paid Off

August 31, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Free Agency Overhaul

When the Blue Bombers entered the 2025 free agency period, they had a clear mandate: replace the departed Stanley Bryant on the offensive line and add speed to a receiving corps that had grown stale. The front office, led by GM Kyle Walters, made Tim White the centrepiece of their receiver overhaul, signing the former Toronto Argonauts standout to a deal worth approximately $280,000 per season. White's impact was immediate — he's been Collaros's most reliable target, and his route-running precision was on full display against Montreal's vaunted secondary, where he consistently found soft spots in zone coverage and turned short throws into chunk gains. Against the Alouettes, White hauled in seven catches for 112 yards and a touchdown, the kind of production the Bombers had been missing from their receiving corps since the departure of Dalton Schoen.

But the most underrated signing was Major Williams, a versatile defensive back who came over from the BC Lions on a two-year deal worth roughly $160,000 per season. Williams has been the eraser in the secondary, and his pre-game comments about the "exciting test" against Montreal's offence proved prophetic — he was all over the field in the 44-28 win, breaking up two passes and providing the physical presence that had been missing in the defensive backfield since the departure of Deatrick Nichols. The Bombers also quietly added Trey Vaval on a one-year minimum deal as a return specialist, a move that paid dividends when Vaval's punt block in the third quarter swung momentum decisively in Winnipeg's favour. That block, recovered by the Bombers at the Montreal 15-yard line, set up a touchdown that turned a tight game into a rout.

The Extension Calculus: Collaros and Oliveira

Winnipeg's cap sheet is dominated by two names: Zach Collaros and Brady Oliveira. Collaros signed a two-year extension in 2024 worth roughly $550,000 per season, a figure that raised eyebrows given his age (36) and the team's cap constraints. But the Bombers bet on his football IQ and leadership, and the bet is paying off — Collaros threw for 320 yards and three touchdowns against Montreal, his best outing of the season, and his red-zone efficiency has improved dramatically since the team simplified the playbook in Week 8. The Bombers' decision to restructure Collaros's deal in the off-season, converting a portion of his base salary into a signing bonus, freed up roughly $80,000 in immediate cap space — a move that allowed them to sign Williams without shedding other assets.

Oliveira, meanwhile, inked a two-year deal at $210,000 per season — a hometown discount that allowed the team to allocate resources elsewhere. The Canadian running back has been the workhorse, averaging 5.2 yards per carry, and his ability to grind out clock in the fourth quarter was critical in the win over the Alouettes. The risk was real: with over 40% of the cap committed to six players, including Willie Jefferson's $200,000-plus deal, the Bombers had little margin for error. Injuries to the O-line early in the season exposed that fragility, leading to a 1-4 start that had fans calling for heads. But the front office held firm, and the recent surge — five wins in six games — suggests the calculus was correct. Jefferson, who was rumoured to be on the trade block in July, has responded with six sacks in the last five games, a reminder that sometimes the best transaction is the one you don't make.

Practice Squad and Injury Wire: The Unsung Moves

The CFL transactions wire tells a story of constant churn. Winnipeg has been one of the most active teams on the practice roster, cycling through defensive linemen and receivers to find depth. The signing of Trey Vaval as a return specialist was a low-cost, high-reward move — Vaval's punt returns and the critical punt block in the second half against Montreal flipped field position and changed the game's momentum. The team also added veteran offensive lineman Chris Kolankowski to the practice squad in Week 6, a move that provided insurance after injuries to starters. Kolankowski, who started 14 games for the Bombers in 2023 before a brief stint in the NFL, was activated in Week 9 and has started the last two games at right guard, stabilizing a unit that had allowed 14 sacks in the first six weeks.

Injury management has also been key. Evan Holm's return from a hamstring injury stabilized the secondary, and the team's decision to place veteran defensive tackle Jake Thomas on the six-game injured list early in the season allowed younger players to develop. The Bombers' medical and personnel staffs have worked in tandem to keep the roster fresh, and it showed in the fourth quarter against Montreal when the defence was still flying to the ball. The team has also been active on the negotiation list, signing three American defensive linemen to futures contracts in September — a clear signal that the front office is already looking ahead to 2026. One of those signings, former NCAA Division II standout Marcus Reed, has already been elevated to the active roster twice and recorded his first career sack against the Alouettes.

The Verdict: A Roster Built for the Stretch Run

At 6-5, the Bombers are far from a lock for the playoffs, but the roster moves are finally coalescing. The White signing, the Williams addition, the Vaval special teams boost, and the disciplined cap management around Collaros and Oliveira have created a team that can beat anyone — as Montreal just learned. The question now is whether the front office has one more move in them before the trade deadline to shore up the defensive line, where the team has been thin since the loss of Thomas. If the Bombers can add one more pass rusher, this roster — rebuilt on the fly through a series of calculated transactions — could be the most dangerous team in the West come November.
