Collaros Back at Practice, but Winnipeg's Roster Math Is the Real Story

October 08, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Collaros Window and the Cap Clawback

Zach Collaros was a limited participant at Winnipeg's closed practice on Wednesday, his first on-field work since the Blue Bombers parked him on the six-game injured list two weeks ago with a head injury. That single line on the team's official injury report is the most consequential roster development in the CFL this week — and not for the reason most fans think.

Head coach Mike O'Shea was characteristically noncommittal: "I would say he's improving. You know me, I'll never call it. I'll always give [players] an opportunity (to show they're healthy enough to play)." That is O'Shea's standard deflection, but it papers over a genuine roster-mechanics problem. The six-game IL exists to give clubs salary-cap relief for players expected to miss extended time. Under CFL rules, that relief is clawed back if the player returns before the six-game window closes. Collaros has missed two games. Activating him now — or even in Week 19 — means Winnipeg forfeits the cap savings it banked when it made the designation.

So the Bombers are weighing two things at once: whether their 38-year-old franchise quarterback is medically cleared, and whether they can afford to be right about it. At 7-8 with Calgary coming to town, the answer to the second question may be no.

The numbers still say Collaros gives Winnipeg its best chance. He's 5-4 as a starter, completing 70.6 percent of his passes for 2,204 yards with 11 touchdowns and six interceptions — a completion rate that would rank among the best marks of his career. Over 181 regular-season CFL games he sits at 68.1 percent for 38,187 yards, 227 touchdowns and 123 interceptions, with 274 carries for 1,506 yards and 11 scores. Three Grey Cups. Two Most Outstanding Player awards. The résumé is not the question.

Dru Brown and the Succession Problem Nobody Wants to Name

The question is Dru Brown. Winnipeg has started the 29-year-old in each of the last two games, and he's gone 2-4 as a starter this season, completing 68.1 percent for 1,502 yards, eight touchdowns and seven interceptions. The completion percentage is nearly identical to Collaros. The touchdown-to-interception ratio is not — 1.14 for Brown against 1.83 for Collaros.

That gap is the whole story of Winnipeg's season. Brown moves the ball between the twenties and stalls in the red zone. He's thrown seven picks in roughly two-thirds the passing volume of Collaros, a turnover rate that has quietly cost this team two or three games in a division decided by margins. And yet the Bombers have now invested two full starts in him with the season on the line, which tells you something about how the organization views the Collaros timeline beyond this year.

This is the roster move nobody lists on the transactions wire: the decision to let Brown take live reps in must-win football. It's an audition disguised as an injury replacement.

The Injury Ledger: Oliveira, Gatkuoth, and a Secondary Held Together by Tape

Wednesday's injury report was brutal beyond the quarterback. Running back Brady Oliveira (ankle), defensive lineman Nuer Gatkuoth (foot), and defensive backs Michael Ayers (calf) and Jaylen Smith (hip) were all non-participants. Jonathan Moxey (groin) was a full participant, which is the only piece of good news on the sheet.

Oliveira's absence is the one that should terrify Winnipeg. He is the engine of this offence — the reason play-action works, the reason Collaros has been able to operate at 70.6 percent. If he can't go against Calgary, the Bombers become a one-dimensional passing team with a quarterback who may or may not be cleared and a backup who turns it over. That is not a formula for beating an 8-8 Stampeders team coming off a 37-31 upset of Saskatchewan.

Two defensive backs and a defensive lineman on the same report is a depth problem, not an injury problem. It means the next men up — practice-roster elevations, special-teamers taking defensive snaps — are being asked to hold coverage against a Calgary passing attack that just hung 37 on the Roughriders.

Janusas and the Practice-Roster Churn

Into that gap steps Vyshonne Janusas, signed to the practice roster Wednesday. The 25-year-old Canadian receiver is a fifth-round pick from the 2025 CFL Draft out of Guelph, and his résumé is a tour of the CFL's recycling bin: 10 games as a rookie with Calgary in 2025 (two catches, 14 yards), two catches for 12 yards this season before the Stampeders released him, a free-agent cup of coffee with Saskatchewan in August that produced one game and zero receptions, and a release late last month.

Three teams in fourteen months. That's not a knock on Janusas — it's the reality of Canadian depth in this league. What's interesting is the frame: five-foot-eleven, 229 pounds. That is not a boundary receiver's body. That's a slotback or an H-back, a blocker who can leak out. At Guelph he caught 71 passes for 1,010 yards and eight touchdowns over 16 games and was named team MVP in 2024, and he returned 30 kickoffs for 800 yards and a score.

The kickoff return number is the tell. Winnipeg's practice roster is not a developmental academy right now — it's a special-teams insurance policy. With Ayers and Smith both down, the Bombers need bodies who can cover kicks and survive. Janusas, who averaged 26.7 yards per return in college, fits that job description better than his receiving stats suggest.

What Saturday Actually Decides

Winnipeg hosts Calgary on Saturday, October 10 at 7:00 p.m. EDT, broadcast on TSN in Canada and CFL+ internationally, with radio on 680 CJOB. The Stampeders are 8-8. The Bombers are 7-8.
