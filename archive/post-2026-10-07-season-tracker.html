Collaros or Bust: Winnipeg's 7-8 Blue Bombers Have Run Out of Margin

October 07, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Collaros Clock Is Ticking on Winnipeg's Season

Zach Collaros was a limited participant in Wednesday's practice, per the team's official injury report. That is the entire update. The 38-year-old has missed Winnipeg's last two games with a head injury and has been parked on the six-game injured list since two weeks ago — a designation that buys salary-cap relief but implies an absence longer than the two games he's actually sat.

Mike O'Shea, asked about his quarterback, offered the platitude he always offers: "I would say he's improving. You know me, I'll never call it. I'll always give [players] an opportunity." That is not a timeline. It's a coach protecting himself from a question he cannot answer, because the honest answer — nobody knows when a 38-year-old's head clears — is not one a head coach says out loud with three weeks left.

The numbers say Winnipeg needs him. Collaros is 5-4 as a starter in 2026, completing 70.6 percent of his passes for 2,204 yards, 11 touchdowns and six interceptions. That is not vintage Collaros — the 38,187-yard, 227-touchdown, three-ring resume is a decade deep now — but it is competent quarterbacking in a league that has very little of it. And competence is precisely what the Bombers have been missing.

Dru Brown and the Offense That Stopped Scoring

Dru Brown has started six games and won two. He's completing 68.1 percent for 1,502 yards with eight touchdowns and seven interceptions. Completion percentage is a liar's stat in this offense — a lot of those completions are checkdowns to a running back who isn't there.

That's the real problem. Brady Oliveira, the engine of everything Winnipeg does offensively, was a non-participant in Wednesday's practice with an ankle injury. So were defensive lineman Nuer Gatkuoth (foot) and defensive backs Michael Ayers (calf) and Jaylen Smith (hip). Jonathan Moxey (groin) was a full participant, which is the only good news on the sheet.

Strip the run game and the play-action that comes off it, hand the ball to a backup quarterback, and you get what Montreal did to Winnipeg last week. The Bombers' offensive identity for five years has been "Oliveira, then everything else." When Oliveira is in a walking boot and Collaros is in the protocol, there is no everything else.

The Playoff Math: 7-8 With Nowhere to Hide

Winnipeg sits at 7-8 with three games left. That is not a comfortable place in the West. The Bombers are scrapping for the division's third seed and, failing that, the crossover berth into the East — the safety valve that requires finishing with a better record than the other division's third-place team.

Every game from here is a coin flip with a knife in it. Lose Saturday and the Bombers are 7-9 with two to play, needing help from three or four other buildings. Win, and they're 8-8, tied with Calgary, holding the head-to-head tiebreaker over the team they just beat.

This is the part of the season where Winnipeg's decade of dominance stops being an asset and starts being a weight. Nobody in that locker room has played a meaningless October game in years. Now they're playing one that means everything, and they're doing it without their quarterback, their running back, and half their secondary.

Saturday Night: Calgary Comes In Hot

The Stampeders (8-8) arrive at Princess Auto Stadium on Saturday, October 10 for a 7:00 p.m. EDT kickoff, fresh off a 37-31 upset of the Saskatchewan Roughriders. That's a Calgary team that just proved it can score on a good defense, walking into a building where the home side got crushed by Montreal a week ago.

The matchup to watch is Calgary's passing game against a Winnipeg secondary missing Ayers and Smith. The Bombers have rotated bodies through the defensive backfield all year; asking that group to hold up against a Stampeders offense with momentum is a big ask. On the other side, whoever starts at quarterback — Brown, or a limited Collaros — has to punish a Calgary defense leaky enough to have lost eight games.

Broadcast: TSN in Canada, CFL+ internationally, 680 CJOB on radio in Winnipeg, 770 CHQR in Calgary.

Power Rankings Reality Check

Winnipeg has spent most of the last five years in the top two of any credible CFL power ranking. This week they belong in the bottom half — somewhere in the 5-to-7 range, behind the West's contenders and behind at least one East team that would happily take a crossover berth over a Bombers squad that cannot stay healthy.

The ranking isn't an insult; it's a description. A 7-8 team with a 38-year-old quarterback in concussion protocol, a hobbled All-Star running back, and a defense patching holes in the secondary is exactly what its record says it is.

The Janusas Signing Is a Tell

Buried in Wednesday's news dump: Winnipeg signed Canadian receiver Vyshonne Janusas to the practice roster. The 25-year-old, five-foot-eleven and 229 pounds, was a fifth-round pick out of Guelph in 2025, played 10 games as a rookie with Calgary (two catches, 14 yards), landed in Saskatchewan as a free agent in August, caught two passes for 12 yards in 2026, and was released late last month.

He's a ratio body who can return kicks — 30 kickoff returns for 800 yards and a touchdown in college, plus 71 catches for 1,010 yards and eight scores in 16 games at Guelph, where he was the Gryphons' 2024 MVP. That is not a franchise-altering addition. It's a team hedging on national depth in case the injury report gets worse, which is the most Winnipeg sentence you can write in October 2026.

The Bombers aren't done. But they're running out of Saturdays.
