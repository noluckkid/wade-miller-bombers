The Bombers' Broken Offense: Why Winnipeg's Scheme Can't Win in 2026

September 28, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

Offensive Scheme: The Collaros Conundrum

Through 17 weeks, the Winnipeg Blue Bombers sit at 8-8, clinging to playoff life. The film shows an offense stuck in neutral. Zach Collaros, once the CFL's most efficient deep passer, has seen his yards per attempt plummet to 7.2 (seventh in the league). Offensive coordinator Buck Pierce's scheme relies heavily on play-action and intermediate crossers, but defenses are sitting on routes. Collaros is completing 68.4% of his passes, but 62% of those completions are within 10 yards of the line of scrimmage. The Bombers rank 7th in points per game (22.3) and 8th in red zone touchdown rate (48.1%). The recent signing of 6-foot-5 receiver Jerand Bradley to the practice roster is a direct response: Winnipeg lacks a jump-ball winner in the red zone. Kenny Lawler is a technician, but at 6-foot-2, he's not winning 50-50 balls consistently. Bradley's 241-pound frame could be a red-zone weapon if elevated. The film from their Week 14 loss to B.C. shows three red-zone trips ending in field goals, all due to failed fade routes to Lawler against smaller corners. Bradley would have been a better matchup.

Defensive Front: The Pass Rush Problem

Defensive coordinator Richie Hall's unit has been the team's backbone, allowing 24.1 points per game (5th). But the film reveals a fatal flaw: they can't generate pressure with four. Winnipeg blitzes 38% of the time (second-highest in the CFL), leaving their secondary exposed. Against mobile quarterbacks like Nathan Rourke, who shredded Saskatchewan for 375 yards and four touchdowns in Week 17, the Bombers' man-coverage scheme breaks down when the pocket extends. Their cornerbacks, particularly Demerio Houston, have been targeted on deep crossers. The signings of D.J. Harvey Jr. and Tamon Lynum add depth, but neither is a proven starter. The real issue is the front four: Willie Jefferson is still elite (11 sacks), but the interior rotation lacks push. Opposing quarterbacks have a clean pocket on 54% of dropbacks, per CFL film metrics. That's unsustainable. In Week 16 against Calgary, Jake Maier had a clean pocket on 19 of 28 dropbacks, picking apart the Bombers' zone coverage for 312 yards. Hall's blitz-heavy approach works against immobile passers, but against scramblers, it's a liability.

Play-Calling Tendencies: Predictability on First Down

Winnipeg runs the ball on 58% of first downs, the highest rate in the CFL. Brady Oliveira is a workhorse (1,102 yards, 5.1 YPC), but the predictability is killing drives. Defenses stack the box with eight defenders on 42% of first-down snaps, forcing Collaros into second-and-long. On second down, the Bombers pass 72% of the time, but their average yards to go is 7.8 — third-worst in the league. The film shows a lack of creativity: few jet motions, rare RPOs, and almost no deep shots on first down. When they do take shots, Collaros' deep accuracy (passes 20+ yards) is just 34.6%, down from 45.2% in 2024. The recent signings of offensive linemen Tunde Fatukasi and Jeremiah Crawford suggest the team knows protection is an issue. Collaros has been sacked 32 times (4th most), and pressure up the middle has forced him to check down. In Week 15 against Saskatchewan, the Riders' defensive tackles stunted on 60% of first-down run plays, blowing up three plays for loss. Pierce never adjusted with a screen or draw.

Third Down and Red Zone: The Collaros Checkdown Problem

Winnipeg converts just 38.2% of second downs (7th), and their red-zone efficiency is even worse. Inside the 20, Collaros targets running backs on 28% of passes, the highest rate in the league. That's a scheme issue: Pierce's play designs often have only two vertical routes, forcing Collaros to check down when pressure arrives. The film shows Dalton Schoen and Nic Demski running deep crossers, but Collaros rarely has time to wait for them to clear. The offensive line's struggles are compounded by Collaros' declining mobility. He's scrambled just 14 times all season, down from 32 in 2024. Defenses don't respect his legs, so they drop eight in coverage. The Bombers need to incorporate more bootlegs and sprint-outs to buy time, but Pierce has called only six bootleg passes all year. That's a failure of imagination.

Matchup to Watch: Bombers vs. Lions' Pass Rush

If Winnipeg sneaks into the playoffs, a likely matchup with B.C. looms. The Lions' defensive front, led by Mathieu Betts (14 sacks), feasts on predictable offenses. In their Week 12 meeting, Betts logged two sacks and three pressures, all on first-down play-action. The Bombers' tendency to run on first down plays right into B.C.'s aggressive run blitzes. To have any chance, Pierce must dial up more first-down passes and use Bradley's size in the red zone. Otherwise, Winnipeg's season ends in the first round — again.
