Bombers' Bye-Week Reset: Collaros Return Masks a Roster in Flux

August 11, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Quarterback Transaction That Changes Everything

When Zach Collaros takes the first snap against the Ottawa RedBlacks on Friday, it will be the most significant transaction the Winnipeg Blue Bombers have made in a month. Collaros has not played since July 5, when a hit from Hamilton Tiger-Cats' Reggie Stubblefield knocked him out of a game Winnipeg eventually won. Taylor Elgersma finished that night; Dru Brown then went 3-2 in games he appeared in, including wins over Toronto and Ottawa before consecutive losses to Calgary and B.C. Brown was also banged up in the 35-19 loss to the Lions, so Collaros' return is about stabilizing the entire quarterback room, not just the starting spot.

The roster math is straightforward: Brown drops back to backup, Elgersma slides to third, and Winnipeg no longer has to scheme around a backup's limitations. But it would be a mistake to treat Collaros as a cure-all. The Bombers surrendered 87 points in their last two games, including a franchise-record 377 rushing yards allowed to B.C. That was not a quarterback problem. Collaros returning doesn't fix the defensive line rotation, the special-teams breakdowns, or the red-zone inefficiency that offensive coordinator Tommy Condell called "consistently inconsistent."

The transaction itself also forces a tough roster decision. To make room for Collaros under the active roster limit, someone has to be moved to the practice roster or released. The Bombers have been quiet about that cut, but it will show up on the CFL transactions wire before Friday. That move — and the player chosen to make room — tells you whether the front office believes this roster is healthy or merely patched together.

Broxton and Lawson: More Than Injury Returns

The transaction wire this week also shows right tackle Jarell Broxton and defensive tackle Cam Lawson back at their posts. Broxton's return is critical for Collaros' protection; Lawson's return gives defensive coordinator Jordan Younger a veteran interior presence against an Ottawa team that runs the ball more than it throws. These are actual moves from the injury list to the active roster, and they signal that Winnipeg wants to fix its 4-4 record with internal personnel rather than outside additions.

Yet internal options have limits. Winnipeg did not make a notable external claim on the CFL transactions wire during the bye week. No veteran defensive tackle was signed to shore up a run defense that got gashed for 377 yards. No fresh-legged returner was added to fix a special-teams unit that has made costly mistakes in consecutive losses. Instead, the Bombers chose to reactivate, reinsert, and re-commit to the same core that lost two straight at home. That is a high-stakes bet: if Broxton or Lawson show rust, the depth behind them is thin.

Broxton's return matters most. Collaros was drilled in July, and he needs a clean pocket. If Broxton is not fully ready, the Bombers have no obvious replacement, and the risk of another Collaros injury goes up. Lawson's return is similarly a bet on health. The defensive tackle rotation was overmatched against B.C., and one returning veteran cannot fix that by himself.

The Dime-Back Shuffle and the Quiet Injury Problem

The most telling transaction of the week may not be official yet. Michael Griffin, the usual dime back, is in a walking boot, and second-year Canadian Jaylen Smith has been working in his spot. That is an internal promotion born of injury, not a signing. It gives Winnipeg a younger, faster option in sub-packages, but it also removes a veteran communicator from the secondary at a moment when the defense is trying to simplify after back-to-back losses.

Griffin's status is worth watching on the injury report. If he cannot play Friday, the Bombers will have to decide whether to keep Smith in the dime role or bring in a veteran from the practice roster. The fact that they have not already made that move suggests they believe Smith is ready. But the bye week was the perfect time to make a quiet transaction if they were unsure. They did not. That is either confidence or stubbornness.

There is also a special-teams component. BlueBombers.com noted "some mistakes on special teams" in the last two losses. A bye-week roster move could have addressed that by adding a returner or replacing a coverage player. Instead, the Bombers ran the same names back out. When a team loses two straight and allows 87 combined points, the transaction wire should move. This week, for Winnipeg, it barely twitched.

Cap, Continuity, and the 2026 Clock

All of these moves happen under a salary cap and a ticking clock. The Blue Bombers are 4-4, and their own website admits the season was "about to slip into the abyss." Collaros' return takes pressure off the offense, but his contract and the front office's loyalty to the core that won Grey Cups in 2019 and 2021 limit what Winnipeg can do on the wire. Every dollar committed to a returning veteran is a dollar not spent on a mid-season rental.

Brady Oliveira has posted consecutive 100-yard rushing games, yet the offense still cannot find the end zone with regularity. That is a roster issue as much as a coaching one. The Bombers have bet that their current roster — once healthy — is good enough. The returns of Collaros, Broxton, and Lawson support that bet. The lack of external moves during the bye does not. If Ottawa exposes the same holes that Calgary and B.C. did, the next transaction will not be an activation. It will be a change to the coaching staff or a sell-off of veteran assets before the 2026 trade deadline.
