Bombers' Roster Churn: Winnipeg's 5-5 Start Is a Transaction Problem

August 28, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Offensive Line Revolving Door

When Brady Oliveira stood in the Commonwealth Stadium locker room last week and admitted the Bombers "absolutely, positively" need to find the why behind their inconsistency, the answer was already written in the transaction log. Winnipeg's offensive line — the unit that once defined their dynasty — has been a patchwork all season. The Bombers have used four different starting combinations across their first ten games, with injuries to both tackles forcing the club to cycle through practice squad call-ups and late-window signings. The result: a second half in Edmonton that produced just two first downs and 19 total yards, numbers that aren't an anomaly but a trend. Through ten games, Winnipeg ranks seventh in the CFL in sacks allowed per game, a staggering fall for a franchise that prided itself on protecting Zach Collaros at all costs.

The transaction wire tells the story. In late July, the Bombers signed veteran offensive lineman Chris Kolankowski off the retired list — a desperation move that signaled the depth chart was broken. Then came the August 12 addition of American tackle Bryce Notree from the practice squad, followed by the release of Canadian guard Liam Dobson on August 19. Each move was a band-aid, not a fix. The front office, led by GM Kyle Walters, has been playing whack-a-mole with a unit that needs continuity, not churn. Collaros, at 36, has been sacked 24 times this season — on pace for his highest total since 2019 — and the pressure is compounding his already erratic decision-making.

The Defensive Backfield Carousel

The secondary has been equally unstable. When Major Williams told bluebombers.com that "everyone is asking who we are," he was speaking literally — the defensive backfield has had more lineup changes than any position group on the roster. Cornerback Jamal Parker went on the six-game injured list on August 8 with a hamstring strain, forcing the club to elevate American defensive back Tyrique McGhee from the practice squad. Two weeks later, safety Nick Hallett was placed on the one-game injured list with a concussion, and the Bombers responded by signing former Alouettes defensive back Najee Murray off the open market — a move that raised eyebrows given Murray's age (31) and his release from Montreal's training camp just weeks earlier.

The numbers back up the chaos. Winnipeg's defence has allowed 30-plus points in four of their last six games, and their 28.4 points-per-game allowed ranks fifth in the league — a far cry from the dominant units that anchored their Grey Cup runs. The Alouettes, who have yet to be held under 30 points this season, are the worst possible matchup for a secondary still learning each other's tendencies. Evan Holm's "exciting opportunity" framing is coach-speak for a group that's been burned deep repeatedly. Montreal's Davis Alexander has completed 68% of his passes of 20-plus yards against Winnipeg's zone coverages, and the Bombers' inability to generate turnovers — just 11 takeaways, tied for sixth — is a direct consequence of a defensive backfield that's too busy communicating to capitalize.

Oliveira's Frustration and the Cap Crunch

Brady Oliveira's post-game comments weren't just about execution — they were about a running back watching his prime years get wasted. Oliveira, who signed a two-year extension worth $210,000 per season in February, has been the Bombers' most consistent offensive weapon, averaging 5.1 yards per carry. But his workload has become unsustainable: he's touched the ball 22-plus times in six of ten games, a pace that risks injury and reflects an offence with no other reliable options. The Bombers' receiving corps, decimated by the offseason departures of Dalton Schoen and Kenny Lawler, has failed to stretch the field, and the club's 6.8 yards per pass attempt ranks ninth in the CFL.

The cap situation compounds the problem. Winnipeg committed over 38% of their 2025 salary cap to six players — Collaros, Oliveira, Willie Jefferson, Adam Bighill, Stanley Bryant, and Jackson Jeffcoat — leaving little room for mid-season upgrades. When the Bombers tried to add depth at receiver in August, they were forced to release Canadian defensive lineman Cameron Lawson to free up cap space, a move that weakened their already-thin Canadian rotation. The practice squad has become a revolving door of short-term fixes: the club has made 14 transactions involving practice squad players since August 1, more than any other West Division team. That's not a strategy; it's a symptom.

The Wire Won't Save Them

With eight games remaining, including two against the Alouettes and five of their last seven against West Division opponents, the Bombers' playoff hopes hinge on whether the transaction wire can stabilize a roster that's been in flux since training camp. The August 25 signing of American defensive end Malik Reed — a former NFL player with 12 career sacks — was a positive step, but it doesn't address the offensive line or the secondary. The Bombers need their existing players to get healthy and their new additions to gel quickly, a tall order against a Montreal team that's won seven straight and is averaging 34.2 points per game.

The harsh truth is that Winnipeg's 5-5 record is exactly what their roster management deserves. The front office has been reactive, not proactive, and the result is a team that looks like a contender in the first half and a pretender in the second. Oliveira's plea for "two halves" is really a plea for a roster that doesn't need to be rebuilt every week. The transaction wire doesn't lie — and right now, it's telling the Bombers they're not good enough.
