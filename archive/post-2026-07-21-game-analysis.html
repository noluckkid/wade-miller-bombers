Dru Brown’s Revenge: How Winnipeg’s Scheme Shift Saved a Sputtering Offence

The First-Half Sputter: Inside Winnipeg’s Run-Heavy Stagnation

Through the first 30 minutes, the Blue Bombers’ offence looked like a relic of 2021. They ran the ball on 12 of their first 18 first-down plays, per CFL Stats, gaining just 3.2 yards per carry. Brady Oliveira was held to 28 yards on nine carries in the half, as Ottawa stacked the box with seven defenders on 70% of those snaps. The Redblacks’ defensive coordinator, Barron Miles, used a 3-4 front with linebackers Adarius Pickett and Tyron Vrede shadowing Oliveira’s cutback lanes. The result: Winnipeg punted on four of six first-half drives, managing only a Sergio Castillo field goal. The Bombers’ play-action game, which typically opens up deep shots, was neutered because Ottawa’s secondary—led by cornerback Brandin Dandridge—refused to bite on fakes, staying in zone shells that limited Kenny Lawler to one catch for 12 yards before halftime.

The Fourth-Quarter Flip: Spread Formations and Vertical Stretches

Trailing 26-12 entering the fourth quarter, offensive coordinator Buck Pierce abandoned the run-heavy script. Winnipeg went to 11 personnel (three receivers, one back, one tight end) on 14 of 17 snaps in the final frame, per game film review. Dru Brown completed 9 of 12 passes for 134 yards and two touchdowns in the quarter, targeting the seams between Ottawa’s linebackers and safeties. The key adjustment: Brown began using play-action from the shotgun, freezing the linebackers just long enough for Nic Demski to run a post-corner route for a 28-yard touchdown at 8:14 of the fourth. On the next drive, Brown hit Pokey Wilson on a slant-and-go that caught cornerback Justin Howell flat-footed—Wilson’s 22-yard score came with Ottawa in Cover 2, leaving the deep middle exposed. The Redblacks’ pass rush, which had sacked Brown twice in the first half, managed only one pressure in the fourth quarter as Brown’s quick release (average 2.3 seconds, per CFL Game Film) neutralized blitzes.

The CFL’s new overtime format—where teams run alternating two-point conversion attempts after the first two possessions—became the decisive battleground. If not for Ottawa’s red-zone ineptitude (they scored touchdowns on just 2 of 6 trips inside the 20), this game would have been a blowout loss.
