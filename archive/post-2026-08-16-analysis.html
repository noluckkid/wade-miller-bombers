Bombers' Roster Chess: Winnipeg's Mid-Season Moves Will Decide Their Fate

August 16, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Collaros Activation Was Only the Beginning

Winnipeg's 33-21 win over the Ottawa RedBlacks Friday night stopped a two-game home skid and moved the Blue Bombers to 5-4, alone in third place in the West Division. The immediate narrative, fairly, was the return of quarterback Zach Collaros. He provided the spark that had been missing while he worked through injury, and the offence produced 33 points against a winless Ottawa team that has now dropped 15 straight games, the longest losing skid in the CFL since the 1948-49 Hamilton Wildcats.

But here is the uncomfortable truth: Collaros's return was not a cure. It was a roster transaction disguised as a football story. The Bombers activated him from the six-game injured list during a week in which the CFL transaction wire was flooded with Winnipeg moves. That is because the teams that win in November are not necessarily the most talented ones; they are the ones that make the right cuts, signings and practice-roster shuffles in August and September. The Bombers are being tested in exactly that way right now.

The Transaction Wire Is the Real Depth Chart

Look at what Winnipeg has endured to get to this point. The defence has had to cycle through bodies in the secondary and at linebacker at a rate that should alarm any fan who believes the roster is set. David Reese, who produced his first career interception Friday night, was on the practice roster earlier in the season. He is now an important piece of the defensive rotation, but his emergence came only after the coaching staff was forced to work through multiple injury designations and short-term signings at the position.

The moves that define a season are rarely the headline signings. They are the veteran defensive back released on a Tuesday afternoon, the American receiver signed to the practice roster and elevated for a week, the Canadian offensive lineman added to keep the ratio functional. Winnipeg has done all of that over the past month. The front office has been aggressive precisely because the roster has not been stable. Nic Demski, who caught six passes for 79 yards Friday, said the team still has "untapped potential." That is true, but it is also true that potential means nothing if the transaction wire keeps eating the depth chart.

The Quarterback Depth and the Oliveira Factor

Carrying three quarterbacks into the season looked like a luxury at Cal Murphy's training camp. It has turned out to be essential. When Collaros went down, Winnipeg did not have to panic because the roster already included a reliable short-yardage and emergency starter in Chris Streveler. That decision — keeping a quarterback allocation rather than using that roster spot for another defensive lineman or receiver — was a quiet but crucial transaction before the season ever started.

The bigger long-term question is Brady Oliveira. Winnipeg's starting running back is the engine of the offence, and his post-game comments Friday — "I don't really care about that. We won." — were classic Oliveira. But the roster reality is that Oliveira is scheduled to become a free agent in February 2027, and the Bombers have not yet announced an extension. Every dollar they commit before the trade deadline is a dollar they cannot use in October. If Winnipeg is serious about contending with Edmonton, Montreal and Saskatchewan, it needs Oliveira. But signing him to a market-value extension while also trying to add a pass rusher or a defensive back before the CFL trade deadline requires cap creativity the front office has not yet shown.

What the Next Four Games Will Expose

The schedule ahead is a meat grinder. The Bombers travel to Edmonton to face a 6-3 Elks team with a defence that has been one of the league's most physical units. Then comes Montreal, the powerhouse of the East, followed by the annual Labour Day Classic and Banjo Bowl against the 6-2 Saskatchewan Roughriders. That is four games in 19 days against three teams that all believe they can win the Grey Cup.

Roster depth will decide at least one of those games. Winnipeg's defensive front can survive a short injury, but it cannot survive two. The secondary cannot afford another six-game injured list designation. The practice roster must be stocked with players who can be elevated and play 25 snaps without embarrassing themselves. The Bombers have been active on the wire, but activity is not the same as quality. They have made moves. The question is whether those moves were made with enough foresight.

The good news for Winnipeg is that the win over Ottawa stopped the bleeding. The bad news is that the RedBlacks are 0-9 and have lost 15 in a row — beating them proved only that the Bombers can beat the worst team in the league. The real test is Edmonton, Montana, Saskatchewan and Saskatchewan again. That test will be won or lost on the transaction wire as much as on the field.

Verdict: A Five-and-Four Team with No Margin for Error

Winnipeg is not a bad team, but it is also not yet a contender. The 33-21 win was a reprieve, not a transformation. The roster has holes: the pass rush remains inconsistent, the secondary is thin, and the offence still leaves points on the board in the red zone. The transaction wire is not going to fix all of that in one week.

What it can do is buy time. The Bombers need to find one more impact player before the deadline. They need to keep Oliveira, keep Collaros upright and keep the national-international ratio balanced through the annual wave of injuries. If the front office wins the next month of transactions, the Blue Bombers could still host a playoff game. If it does not, the September schedule will turn those close losses into a season-ending spiral. Friday's win was a step in the right direction, but the real game of chess is just beginning.
