The SAM Shuffle: Winnipeg's Roster Patchwork Is a Playoff Red Flag

October 16, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The SAM Shuffle: A Position Without an Owner

Winnipeg's Thursday depth chart delivered a single change, and it says more about this season than any box score will. Jaylen Smith, the 24-year-old Hamilton native and second-round pick out of North Texas in the 2025 CFL Draft, has been moved to the one-game injured list with a hip injury suffered last week against Calgary. He did not practise all week and was listed as questionable on the club's final injury report. Seven starts at strong-side linebacker, 46 total tackles, and now a seat on the sideline in Week 20 with a playoff spot on the line.

Michael Griffin II slides in. This will be his ninth start at SAM this season — his third year in Winnipeg — to go with 39 defensive tackles, one special teams tackle, one sack and one forced fumble. Over 51 career regular-season games split between the Blue Bombers and Stampeders, he has 139 defensive tackles, 31 special teams tackles, four sacks, four pass knockdowns, one forced fumble and one fumble recovery.

Do the arithmetic: 16 games, 16 starts at SAM, split 9-7 between two players. That is not a platoon. That is a position the defensive staff has never settled on. Griffin is the veteran stabilizer who keeps losing the job; Smith is the rookie who keeps winning it and keeps getting nicked. When your strong-side linebacker is a coin flip week to week, your coverage rules, your run-fit leverage and your blitz packages all shift with it. Continuity at SAM is not a luxury — it is the hinge between the front seven and the secondary.

Novak and the Ratio Math

Smith's roster spot went to Lane Novak, promoted off the practice roster. The Balgonie, Sask. product was a fifth-round pick in the 2025 CFL Draft and has played 10 regular-season games with four special teams tackles. On paper, a footnote. In practice, the ratio talking.

Smith is Canadian. Novak is Canadian. When a national starter comes off the board, the roster has to be rebalanced somewhere, and the cheapest place to do it is a special-teams body who can dress without forcing a domino through the starting 12 on either side of the ball. Novak's four special-teams tackles in 10 games is the entire résumé — and that is precisely the point. He is not being elevated to play SAM. He is being elevated so the math works.

That is a real cost. Every October practice-roster promotion is a bet that your developmental pipeline is deep enough to absorb an injury without changing what you call on defence. Winnipeg is making that bet in a must-win week. The Bombers are not the only club doing ratio gymnastics in Week 20 — Ottawa is starting former NFL receiver Grant DuBose in place of Justin Hardy at slotback, and Toronto has handed Cam Porter the running back job with Ali Fayad at defensive end — but the Redblacks and Argos have nothing to play for. Winnipeg does.

Bad Timing, Worse History: Edmonton at Week 20

Winnipeg sits at 8-8. A win clinches a playoff spot. Standing in the way is the one opponent that has owned them: Edmonton, 2-0 against the Bombers this season, in a West Division where three teams are fighting for the last two playoff berths.

The Week 4 loss — 23-18 at Princess Auto Stadium — was self-inflicted. Four first-half fumbles. You do not lose many games where the opponent does not beat you; you lose the ones where you beat yourself, and four giveaways before halftime is a systemic ball-security problem, not bad luck.

The Week 12 loss was worse in a different way. Brady Oliveira ran for 118 yards at Commonwealth Stadium and Winnipeg still lost 22-19. When your bell-cow back clears a hundred and you do not win, the failure is downstream — red-zone conversion, situational play-calling, or a defence that cannot get off the field in the fourth quarter. Both losses came with a healthy Smith in the lineup, incidentally. The injury is not the reason Winnipeg is 0-2 against Edmonton. It is the reason the margin for error just got thinner.

What the Wire Actually Says

Strip the noise and this week's transaction wire says three things about the Blue Bombers. One: the club is healthy enough to make exactly one change, which is better than most teams in Week 20. Two: the change lands on the least stable position on the roster. Three: the replacement body is a ratio patch, not a football answer.

None of that is fatal. Griffin has started nine games at SAM this year and knows the system; he is not a liability, he is a known quantity. Novak can run down kicks. The Bombers can win Friday and clinch, and the depth chart will look fine on paper.

But the pattern matters more than the week. A team that has spent 16 games auditioning two players at one linebacker spot, and that reaches into its practice roster to solve a national-ratio problem in a playoff-deciding game, is telling you something about how thin the margin has become. Winnipeg's core is still good enough to make the postseason. Whether it is deep enough to survive it is a different question — and the answer starts at SAM.
