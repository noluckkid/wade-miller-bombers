The Bombers' Roster Roulette: Winnipeg's Transaction Wire Tells a Troubling Story

September 08, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Secondary Shuffle

The Labour Day Classic loss to Saskatchewan was a microcosm of the Winnipeg Blue Bombers' 2025 season: a defence that held up for 45 minutes, then collapsed when it mattered most. But the deeper story is in the roster moves that got them there. Ridge Texada, dressed for only his second career CFL game, was targeted repeatedly by Trevor Harris and the Roughriders' offence. He got fooled on a double-move that set up a touchdown, then gave up a bust in coverage two plays later. This is what happens when a team is cycling through defensive backs like a revolving door.

The Bombers have dressed 11 different defensive backs this season, a staggering number for a team that prides itself on defensive stability. Jaiden Woodbey, who missed a tackle on a 72-yard catch-and-run from Daniel Wiebe, was signed off the practice squad in July. Evan Holm, who took a costly face-masking penalty that extended a touchdown drive, was claimed off waivers from the BC Lions in June. These are not the moves of a team with a plan; they are the moves of a team in survival mode.

The Quarterback Conundrum

Zach Collaros is 38 years old, playing with a nagging neck injury that has already cost him four games this season. When he's on the field, he's still effective — 140-of-196 for 1,750 yards, eight touchdowns and four interceptions, a 71.4 percent completion rate. But the Bombers' offence sits seventh in the CFL in net yardage, yards per play and passing. The numbers don't lie: this is not the same Collaros who won back-to-back Grey Cups.

Offensive coordinator Tommy Condell's "keys to the Maserati" comment is revealing. "He has the keys to the Maserati, so to speak, and I'm not asking him to come home by 12," Condell told reporters. It's a nice sentiment, but it also signals that the Bombers have no viable alternative. When Collaros went down with the neck injury, the team cycled through two backup quarterbacks — neither of whom inspired confidence. The transaction wire shows the Bombers signed and released three quarterbacks in August alone, a desperate scramble that suggests the front office knows the backup situation is untenable.

The Cap Crunch

Here's where the roster moves get expensive. Collaros is earning a base salary of $550,000 this season, and the Bombers have committed over 30 percent of their salary cap to the quarterback position. That leaves little room for the kind of mid-season signings that shore up a secondary in crisis. The team's decision to release veteran defensive back Deatrick Nichols in July — a cap-saving move that freed up $180,000 — looks increasingly shortsighted given the revolving door that has followed.

The Bombers are also paying for past glory. Defensive end Willie Jefferson, at 34, is earning $300,000 this season but has just four sacks through nine games. Linebacker Adam Bighill, 36, is making $250,000 and has visibly lost a step in coverage. These are legacy contracts that the front office can't escape, and they're squeezing the cap space needed for younger, cheaper talent. The result is a roster that's top-heavy with aging veterans and thin at the positions that matter most in a long CFL season.

The Practice Squad Pipeline

The Bombers' practice squad has become a de facto extension of the active roster. In the last month alone, the team has elevated six players from the practice squad to the active roster, including two defensive backs and a defensive lineman. This is not a sustainable model. Practice squad players are developmental projects, not starters. When you're relying on them to play meaningful snaps in a Labour Day Classic, you're admitting that your depth chart is broken.

The team's scouting department deserves some credit for finding serviceable bodies — Texada, for all his struggles, was an undrafted free agent who has shown flashes. But the Bombers' front office has been reactive rather than proactive. The CFL transactions wire shows a team making moves out of desperation, not design. Every signing is a response to an injury or a poor performance, not a strategic addition to the roster.

The Verdict

The Bombers are 4-5, sitting third in the West Division, and the Labour Day Classic loss exposed the structural problems that the transaction wire has been telegraphing all season. The secondary is a patchwork, the quarterback is playing hurt with no reliable backup, and the cap is weighed down by aging veterans. This is not a team that's one or two moves away from contention. This is a team that needs a fundamental roster reset.

The front office has some tough decisions ahead. Do they move on from Jefferson and Bighill after this season, eating the dead cap hit to free up space? Do they draft a quarterback in 2026 to develop behind Collaros? Do they invest in the secondary through free agency rather than the waiver wire? These are the questions that will define the next era of Bombers football. The answers, so far, have been anything but encouraging.
