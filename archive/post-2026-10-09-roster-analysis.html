Collaros Returns, But Winnipeg's Roster Churn Exposes a Deeper Crack

October 09, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Collaros Calculus: A Band-Aid on a Fractured Season

Zach Collaros will start Saturday against the Calgary Stampeders, ending a two-game absence caused by a head injury. The 38-year-old returned to practice this week and, per 3DownNation, publicly reaffirmed his decision to keep playing after what he described as careful consideration. That sentence should alarm anyone who has watched this team closely. When a quarterback has to reassure the fanbase that he still wants to play, the roster is no longer being managed — it is being triaged.

The numbers tell the story of a season held together with tape. Collaros is 5-4 as a starter, completing 70.6 percent of his passes for 2,204 yards, 11 touchdowns, and six interceptions. Those are functional numbers. But he has missed six regular-season games in 2025, and the Bombers have now burned through a two-game stretch where the offense was handed to Dru Brown. Brown remains the backup, Bryce Perkins holds the third-string role, and Taylor Elgersma — the developmental arm this franchise spent real draft capital on — has been moved to the reserve roster.

That last move is the tell. Winnipeg is carrying three veteran quarterbacks and zero developmental runway on the active roster. Elgersma on the reserve list means the Bombers have effectively punted on grooming a successor in-season. This is a front office saying the only thing that matters is the next 60 minutes. It is defensible when you are 12-2. It is far more questionable when your starter has missed 40 percent of the schedule and is one hit away from a Brown-Perkins two-man room.

Centre of Attention: Eli In, Elsbury Out

The most consequential non-quarterback move is at centre, where Tui Eli replaces Tyler Elsbury, who slides to a depth role. The trigger was last week's blowout loss to Montreal, in which Elsbury took a holding penalty, delivered a bad snap, and allowed a sack. That is a three-error afternoon at the position that touches the ball on every single snap. In a league where the centre-quarterback exchange is sacred, that is not a slump — it is a liability.

What makes this move revealing is the history. Eli made one start at centre earlier this season, and it only happened because Elsbury shifted to right guard in place of Patrick Neufeld. In other words, the Bombers' preferred configuration all year has included Elsbury somewhere on the interior. Now he is off the starting five entirely. That is a coaching staff admitting its preferred configuration was wrong.

Eli, 30, is no project. He has played 85 career regular-season CFL games at centre and guard since being taken in the fourth round of the 2019 CFL Draft out of the University of Hawaii. He is a known quantity — steady, assignment-sound, unspectacular. In October, with a 38-year-old quarterback coming off a head injury, steady is exactly what you buy. The Bombers are trading upside for reliability at the one position where a bad snap can end a season.

Linebacker and Edge: Smith Returns, Jenkins Gets the Yo-Yo

Jaylen Smith returns to start at strong-side linebacker after a hip injury kept him out last week. The Hamilton native has 43 total tackles this season and this will be his seventh start. That is a tackle rate north of six per game from the SAM spot — genuine production, not depth-chart filler. Michael Griffin II, who started in his place last week, slides back to a depth role. That is a clean upgrade, and the kind of move a contender should make without hesitation.

The Kydran Jenkins transaction is messier. The 24-year-old defensive end has been promoted to the active roster in a depth role. He started the first three games of the regular season, tallied four defensive tackles, and was moved to the practice squad. Now he is back. Four tackles across three starts is not a résumé — it is a placeholder. The promotion is about bodies, not merit.

This is the practice-squad yo-yo in its purest form, and it exposes Winnipeg's lack of edge depth. When a team cycles a player between the active roster and the practice squad three times in a season, it is not developing him. It is renting him. Jenkins is being asked to absorb snaps so that the starters can breathe, and the moment a healthier body appears, he will be back on the PR. That is not a knock on Jenkins. It is an indictment of how thin the defensive front has become.

The Transaction Wire Tells the Real Story

Zoom out and the pattern across this week's CFL transactions is unmistakable. Winnipeg made three starting-lineup changes, one reserve-roster demotion, one practice-squad promotion, and one depth-chart relegation — all in the span of a single depth chart release. That is not a team fine-tuning. That is a team searching.

The West Division race remains wide open in Week 19, with all five teams in action and every playoff spot still live. In that environment, the Bombers' margin for error is razor-thin, and their roster management reflects it. They are prioritizing proven veterans over developmental projects, plugging leaks at centre and SAM, and cycling edge bodies through the practice squad because there is no better option on the shelf.

The Collaros decision fits the same logic. You do not hand the season to Dru Brown when the division is this tight and your starter — even at 38, even with six missed games — gives you a 70.6 percent completion rate and a 5-4 record. You ride the veteran and you patch everything around him.

But patching has a cost. Elgersma's development stalls. Jenkins never gets a real runway. The interior offensive line has now started three different configurations in a single season. Winnipeg is winning the week. The question is whether they are losing the next two years to do it. Saturday against Calgary will not answer that. But the transaction wire already has.
