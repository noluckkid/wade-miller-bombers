Winnipeg's Transaction Treadmill: How Broxton's Injury Triggered a Roster Overhaul

July 30, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Broxton Blow: Offensive Line Depth Tested

When Jarell Broxton went down with a torn pectoral muscle in the loss to Calgary, Winnipeg’s offensive line didn’t just lose a starter—it lost its most versatile piece. Broxton had started all seven games at right tackle and had been the anchor of a unit that allowed only 12 sacks through six weeks. On July 3, the Bombers placed him on the 6-game injured list, a move that triggered a cascade of transactions revealing just how thin the protection pipeline really is.

Immediately, the club elevated 6-foot-5, 336-pound Micah Vanterpool from the practice squad—a former third-grade teacher who has been “chopping wood” since 2024, as he described his wait. Vanterpool started six games last year at left guard and left tackle, but now he flips to the right side, a switch head coach Mike O’Shea acknowledged is “not ideal” on a short week. To backfill the practice roster, Winnipeg signed former Ticats tackle Daymond Williams and added University of Alberta product Kendall Randolph, a 27-year-old who hasn’t played a regular-season snap since 2023. The result is a line that features two starters (Vanterpool and left guard Liam Dobson) who began the season outside the starting unit, and a depth chart that lists just one healthy reserve with CFL experience, Geoff Gray, himself a mid-June signing after being cut by Saskatchewan.

Quarterback Turmoil Multiplies the Transaction Count

If the offensive line troubles weren’t enough, Winnipeg’s quarterback situation generated a parallel wave of roster moves. After Zach Collaros suffered a concussion in Week 5, the Bombers placed him on the 6-game injured list on July 5, a demoralizing blow that left them with only two healthy arms: sophomore Dru Brown and global draft pick Taylor Elgersma. Elgersma, a 22-year-old from the Netherlands, made his first CFL start in Week 6 and looked overwhelmed, prompting the club to turn back to Brown for the crucial home date against B.C.

What the public didn’t see was the whirlwind behind the pivot shuffle. On July 6, the team signed quarterback Chris Merchant, released by the Stampeders in training camp, to the practice squad—a move that required cutting receiver Jaylen Hall from the developmental roster to stay under the 10-man practice limit. Then on July 8, sensing Elgersma’s confidence needed a reset, the Bombers moved the global quarterback to the practice squad while promoting receiver Brendan O’Leary-Orange for special teams, leaving Brown as the only QB on the active depth chart. The cap implications are stark: Collaros’s $500,000 salary sits on injured reserve against the SMS, while Brown’s $85,000 bargain deal keeps the offence afloat. In eight days, the quarterback position alone accounted for six transactions—two signings, three roster moves, and one injury designation.

Defensive Patchwork: Castoffs Become Starters

While the offensive chaos draws attention, Winnipeg’s defence has quietly undergone its own makeover. When weakside linebacker Kyrie Wilson landed on the 6-game injured list in Week 4 with a knee sprain, the Bombers turned not to a drafted rookie but to 28-year-old Malik Clements, signed from the free-agent pool on June 19 after Toronto cut him at camp. Clements promptly started in a Week 5 win over Ottawa, recording six tackles and a forced fumble—a testament to the organization’s scouting of other teams’ discards. A week later, to boost pass-rush depth, the club released global defensive end Thiadric Hansen and signed former AAF standout Jeremiah Valoaga, who had been out of football since 2023, to a one-year minimum deal ($71,000). The move cleared a cap-friendly spot on the active roster and gave defensive coordinator Jordan Younger a rotational body behind Willy Jefferson.

The transactions log underscores the interdependency: Winnipeg has made seven moves involving defensive players since June 20, including releasing Canadian defensive tackle David Green to make room for receiver O’Leary-Orange—a domino effect from the quarterback decisions. With Jefferson nursing a sore hamstring, the Bombers have used three different defensive ends in four weeks, none of whom were with the team in May. The special-teams unit felt the ripple when return man Janarion Grant was released in late June to free SMS space for these defensive signings, forcing Nic Demski into return duties.

The Practice Squad as a Survival Mechanism

Across the CFL, teams use the practice squad to develop young talent, but Winnipeg in 2025 has turned it into a triage unit. Through Week 7, the Bombers have made 22 transactions involving their practice roster—more than any other West Division club. As of Thursday’s game, seven of the 45 players on the active and practice rosters were not with the team in training camp, including two starting offensive linemen, the backup quarterback, and a starting linebacker.
