The Bombers' Roster Shuffle: Wilson and Griffin Return, But at What Cost?

October 02, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Receiver Room Gets Its Third Option Back

Ontaria Wilson's return to the starting lineup for Saturday's visit to Montreal is the single most consequential line on Winnipeg's Week 18 depth chart. The 27-year-old from Ashburn, Georgia has caught 56 passes for 739 yards and five touchdowns across 12 regular-season games, a pace that slots him third in the Bombers' target hierarchy behind Tim White and Nic Demski. That is not a luxury. It is a structural requirement for an offence that has spent the back half of this season patching holes in its receiving corps.

Wilson missed the last two games with a hand injury. In his absence, Winnipeg's passing attack compressed into a two-man operation, and the coverage math got easier for opposing defensive coordinators. A hand injury is particularly punitive for a receiver — it compromises release at the line of scrimmage, it compromises the catch point, and it compromises the ability to fight through contact on contested balls. The fact that he is back on the depth chart rather than parked on the one-game injured list suggests the Bombers believe he can contribute immediately rather than merely occupy a roster spot.

The corresponding move is the one that tells you where the organization's priorities sit. Kenneth Womack, a rookie who dressed for Winnipeg's last two games without registering a single statistic, has been demoted to the practice roster. That is not a knock on Womack so much as an admission about what the Bombers need right now: proven production over developmental reps. With 36 career regular-season games already on his resume — 148 receptions, 2,147 yards, 11 touchdowns — Wilson is a known quantity. Womack is not. In October, with playoff positioning on the line, known quantities win.

Griffin's Return and the Ratio Squeeze

Michael Griffin II's return to the starting lineup at strong-side linebacker is the more complicated transaction, because it triggers a cascade through the Canadian ratio. Griffin II was Winnipeg's day-one starter at the position before an ankle injury cost him six games. In that window he accumulated 35 defensive tackles, one special teams tackle, and one forced fumble. The six-foot, 231-pound defender is in his fourth CFL season and has now appeared in 49 career regular-season games split between Winnipeg and Calgary, with 135 defensive tackles, 31 special teams tackles, one tackle for loss, four pass knockdowns, three sacks, one forced fumble, one fumble recovery, and one touchdown to his name.

Those are starter's numbers. But the mechanism of his return matters more than the numbers themselves. Jaylen Smith, the Canadian defender who filled in during Griffin II's absence, has been moved to the reserve roster — and he was already listed as questionable with a hip injury after sitting out Thursday's practice as a non-participant. That is a double signal. First, Winnipeg is not carrying a healthy scratch; it is carrying an injured player it cannot use. Second, the Bombers are choosing to absorb the ratio consequences of starting an American at strong-side linebacker rather than dress a compromised national.

That decision has downstream costs. Every American starter at a ratio-relevant position forces a Canadian into the lineup elsewhere, and the Bombers have spent this season shuffling that arithmetic. Smith's move to the reserve roster — not the practice roster, which matters — keeps him nominally available while freeing the active spot. It is a hedge, and hedges are what October rosters are made of.

Hansen, Stuart, and the Depth Calculus

The two additions at the bottom of the depth chart deserve more attention than they will get. Thiadric Hansen, a Global defensive lineman, recently rejoined the team's practice roster as a free agent and now gets an active roster spot in a depth role. The 33-year-old native of Flensburg, Germany was the second overall pick in the 2019 CFL Global Draft and has appeared in 69 career regular-season CFL games, recording 22 defensive tackles and 40 special teams tackles.

Read that ratio again: 40 special teams tackles against 22 defensive tackles. Hansen is, functionally, a core special-teamer who can take a rotational snap on the defensive line when the ratio allows. For a team entering the stretch run, that is a specific and useful skill set — and it is the kind of move that costs nothing against the cap and buys insurance against an in-game injury at a position where Winnipeg has historically been thin.

Ethan Stuart, a Canadian defensive back, gets the same treatment in a depth role. Canadian depth in the secondary is the most valuable currency in the CFL's ratio economy, because it lets a coordinator be flexible with his American defensive backs without burning a designated import spot. Neither Hansen nor Stuart will decide Saturday's game. Both of them exist so that a single injury on Saturday does not decide it either.

What the Churn Says About Winnipeg's Window

Step back from the individual moves and a pattern emerges. Winnipeg's Week 18 transactions are almost entirely reactive: an injured receiver returns, an injured linebacker returns, an injured Canadian gets shelved, two depth bodies get promoted. There is no forward-looking acquisition here, no trade for a difference-maker, no signing designed to raise the ceiling. This is a roster being managed for availability, not for upside.

That is not necessarily a criticism. In a league where the gap between second and fourth in a division is often two plays, availability is a strategy. But it does tell you something about where the Bombers believe they are. Teams with margin make aggressive moves in October. Teams without margin make moves that keep eleven healthy bodies on the field. Winnipeg's depth chart this week reads like the latter.

The test comes Saturday in Montreal.
