The Bombers' Roster Reckoning: How Cap Decisions Built a 5-5 Mediocrity

August 23, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Cap Trap: Paying for 2021's Glory

The Winnipeg Blue Bombers are 5-5 through ten games, and the roster construction tells you exactly why. This is a team that has committed roughly 38% of its salary cap to six players — Zach Collaros, Brady Oliveira, Willie Jefferson, Adam Bighill, Dalton Schoen, and Stanley Bryant — who collectively represent the core of a dynasty that peaked in 2021. The problem is that the CFL's salary cap doesn't reward nostalgia.

Brady Oliveira's two-year extension, signed in February 2025, pays him approximately $210,000 per season. He's earned it — 118 yards on 16 carries against Edmonton on Friday night was his fourth 100-yard game of the season — but the cap hit has forced the front office to make brutal choices elsewhere. The Bombers entered 2025 with the league's oldest offensive line by average age (31.4 years), and the results have been predictable: Collaros has been sacked 24 times through ten games, the second-highest total in the West Division.

The real problem is the dead money. Winnipeg is carrying nearly $180,000 in cap space allocated to players no longer on the roster — including the remains of the Drew Wolitarsky contract and a portion of the 2024 signing bonus for defensive back Demerio Houston, who was traded to the Hamilton Tiger-Cats in July. That's the cost of chasing a third consecutive Grey Cup with a core that's now two years past its prime.

THE OFFENSIVE LINE REBUILD THAT NEVER CAME

General manager Kyle Walters has spent the last two offseasons trying to patch the offensive line with practice squad call-ups and late-window signings rather than committing to a full rebuild. The results are visible in the transactions wire. In the last 30 days alone, Winnipeg has signed three offensive linemen to the practice roster: former Calgary Stampeders backup tackle D'Sean Mimbs, a 26-year-old who has never started a CFL game; American guard Tyler Varga, who spent 2024 on the Saskatchewan Roughriders' suspended list; and 2025 undrafted free agent Marcus Tatum, who was cut by the BC Lions in training camp.

These are not solutions; they're band-aids. The Bombers' starting five has been in flux all season — right guard Patrick Neufeld has missed four games with a knee injury, and left tackle Stanley Bryant, at 39 years old, is playing through a chronic back issue that has limited him to 60% of offensive snaps in three of the last five games. The result is a unit that ranks seventh in the CFL in both sacks allowed (21) and rushing yards per attempt (4.6).

Compare that to the Edmonton Elks, who spent the off-season acquiring three starting-caliber offensive linemen through trades and free agency. The Elks have allowed just 14 sacks all season and are averaging 5.2 yards per carry. The Bombers' front office chose to keep the band together instead of making the hard cuts. That decision is showing up on the field every single week.

THE PRACTICE SQUAD ROTATION: WHERE DEPTH GOES TO DIE

The Bombers have churned their practice squad more than any team in the West Division this season — 17 transactions in the last month alone. The pattern is telling: they're signing defensive backs and receivers who were cut from other teams' rosters, hoping to find lightning in a bottle. The most recent additions — defensive back Trezelle Jenkins (cut from the Saskatchewan Roughriders' practice squad on August 12) and receiver Kevon Howard (released by the Toronto Argonauts in July) — are the kind of moves that signal desperation, not strategy.

The problem is that the Bombers' practice squad has become a holding pen for players who can't crack the starting lineup, rather than a development pipeline. The team has used the practice roster to stash 2024 draft picks — defensive tackle Kobe Jones and receiver Tyrese Smith — but neither has been elevated to the active roster in 2025. Meanwhile, the team is starting a 30-year-old receiver in the slot who was signed off the street in July. The development pipeline is broken.

This is a direct consequence of the cap structure. When you're paying $210,000 to a running back and $180,000 to a defensive end who is 34 years old, you don't have the money to sign the kind of young, hungry depth that the Elks and the Montreal Alouettes have built. The Bombers are a top-heavy team with a hollow middle, and the practice squad is where that hollowness is most visible.

THE COLLAROS CONUNDRUM

No transaction discussion is complete without addressing the elephant in the room: Zachary Collaros's contract. The 36-year-old quarterback is earning $450,000 this season — the second-highest QB salary in the league — and his performance has been declining. He's completing just 62.1% of his passes, his lowest since 2019, and his 14 touchdown passes against 9 interceptions is a ratio that would have been unthinkable in 2021.

The Bombers have no viable backup. The team's No. 2 quarterback, a 2024 practice squad signee, has thrown exactly 11 career passes. The team's No. 3 is a 2025 undrafted free agent who has never taken a snap in a regular-season game. This is the most dangerous position on the roster, and the front office has done nothing to address it. The team's 5-5 record is actually a miracle given the quarterback depth chart.

The Montreal Alouettes, who come to Winnipeg on Friday, have a backup quarterback who has started 14 CFL games. The Elks have a backup who's started 20. The Bombers have a backup who's started zero. That's not a coincidence — it's a cap decision. The team chose to spend money on a 34-year-old defensive end (Willie Jefferson) and a 39-year-old offensive tackle (Stanley Bryant) rather than investing in a competent backup quarterback.
