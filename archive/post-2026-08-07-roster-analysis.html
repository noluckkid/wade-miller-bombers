Bombers' Roster Retool: How Winnipeg's Moves Shaped a 4-4 Start

August 07, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

Defensive Line Decisions Paying Off

When Winnipeg's front office watched its pass rush produce a league-worst 23 sacks in 2025, the response was not a philosophical shift but a transaction. In February 2026, general manager Kyle Walters signed defensive tackle Jake Ceresna to a two-year contract after Ceresna posted 10 sacks in Edmonton. The move, combined with Willie Jefferson's offseason renegotiation and the cap-saving release of Jackson Jeffcoat, has transformed the CFL's most passive front into one of its most aggressive. Through eight games, the Bombers are tied with Saskatchewan for second in sacks with 17, trailing Calgary by one. Jefferson and Ceresna each have six, placing them in a three-way tie for the league lead alongside B.C.'s Mathieu Betts.

The deeper analytics underline the roster shift. Pro Football Focus currently credits Winnipeg with the highest quarterback-pressure rate in the CFL—a staggering spike from 2025, when they ranked last in sacks and near the bottom in pressure. Ceresna's interior push is the obvious catalyst: offensive lines can no longer slide protections solely toward Jefferson, who has doubled his sack total from the same point last year. The defencive line has also absorbed injuries without collapse, thanks to the mid-May signing of veteran tackle Matt Korte from Calgary's practice roster. Korte has played 31% of defensive snaps since Week 3 while spending most of the season on the five-man practice rotation. This is the Bombers' transaction history working exactly as drawn up: targeted free-agent additions, careful cap casualties, and low-cost depth that keeps a pass rush alive during a grueling schedule.

The Oliveira Conundrum: Money, Workload, and Wins

The Bombers' most significant offensive transaction was not a new face but a cemented one. On June 9, Oliveira signed a three-year extension worth $1.5 million in total compensation, making him the highest-paid running back in the CFL. The deal was meant to lock in the team's identity, but the early returns are far more complicated. Oliveira leads the league in rushing yards and carries through eight games, yet his 100-yard performances no longer guarantee victories. From 2021-24, the Bombers went 16-1 in starts when Oliveira topped the century mark. Over the last two seasons, including this one, they are 3-4 in those games.

The contract also appears to have shifted the offensive hierarchy. Oliveira's receiving numbers are down from his career-best 61 catches in 2025 to an on-pace 45 this season. His workload, however, is up—33 carries for 235 yards in the last two games compared to 24 carries for 176 yards in the two prior contests. This is a direct consequence of the team's most controversial transaction: the April trade of former No. 1 receiver Dalton Schoen to Hamilton for conditional draft picks. With Schoen gone, the Bombers have leaned harder into the run and into short passes for Nic Demski, who re-signed on a two-year deal in February. The offense has become more conservative, by design, but the fourth-quarter collapse against B.C. in Week 8—when Winnipeg was outscored 25-3 after holding a 19-13 lead—showed the limits of that philosophy. The cap savings from the Schoen trade ($185,000) helped fund Oliveira's $115,000 roster bonus, but the transaction left the receiving corps thin and defenses able to stack the box in high-leverage situations.

Mid-Season Churn: Injuries, Practice Squad, and the QB Carousel

The Bombers used their second bye week to address a bruising eight-game start. The August 10 transaction wire showed defensive back Evan Holm moving to the six-game injured list with a hamstring strain, with American cornerback Demetrius Harris signed to the practice squad to fill depth. Holm's absence exposes a secondary that has already surrendered three 200-yard passing games. Two weeks earlier, the club released veteran returner Wes Hills following a costly fumble in a loss to Toronto, promoting practice-squad back Miles Green to the active roster. Green, who missed all of 2025 on Edmonton's injury list, has responded with a 23.4-yard kickoff-return average and 15 punt returns without a muff.

The most consequential mid-season move was forced by injury. On July 30, Winnipeg signed quarterback Alex Guz off B.C.'s practice roster after Chris Streveler ruptured his Achilles in the Week 6 loss to Calgary. Streveler's injury left the Bombers with only three quarterbacks in the building. Guz, a former second-round pick at Michigan, has already taken 14 offensive snaps in relief while the team placed Streveler on the season-ending injured list. The Bombers also added defensive lineman Patrick Lowe to the practice squad on August 12, a quiet move aimed at preserving pass-rush depth behind Jefferson, Ceresna, and Korte. With roughly $212,000 in remaining salary cap space, Walters has room for one more significant signing before the trade deadline—likely a defensive back, given the Holm injury and the repeated fourth-quarter lapses that have defined an uneven 4-4 start.
