Winnipeg's October Patch Job: Janusas Signing Exposes a Thin Roster

October 07, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Janusas Signing: Depth Insurance, Not a Fix

On Wednesday, the Winnipeg Blue Bombers added Canadian receiver Vyshonne Janusas to their practice roster. On its face, it's the kind of transaction that normally slides past unnoticed — a 25-year-old national pass-catcher with two career CFL receptions joining a team that sits 7-8 and is fighting for its playoff life. But the timing and the player tell you more about where this roster actually stands than any depth chart will.

Janusas is five-foot-eleven, 229 pounds, a fifth-round pick in the 2025 CFL Draft out of the University of Guelph. He caught 71 passes for 1,010 yards and eight touchdowns over 16 collegiate games with the Gryphons and was named team MVP in 2024. He also returned 30 kickoffs for 800 yards and one touchdown — that last number matters more than the receiving line for a team scraping for special-teams leverage in October.

Three Teams in Twelve Months

The journeyman arc is the real story. Calgary drafted him, gave him 10 regular-season games as a rookie, and got two catches for 14 yards out of it. He caught two more passes for 12 yards in 2026 before the Stampeders cut him. Saskatchewan signed him as a free agent in August, dressed him for one game, watched him record zero receptions, and released him late last month. Now he's in Winnipeg on a practice roster.

That's three organizations in roughly a calendar year for a player who was a collegiate MVP. In the CFL's national-receiver economy, that trajectory is common — but it's also a signal. Teams cycle through Canadian pass-catchers because the ratio forces them to carry bodies, not because any of those bodies are expected to produce. Janusas is ratio insurance with return upside, and the Bombers are buying low on a player whose draft pedigree still has a little shine left.

The Real Problem Isn't at Receiver

Here's the uncomfortable part for Winnipeg: the position they just reinforced is not the one that got them embarrassed. The Bombers were crushed by Montreal in their most recent outing, and the damage came through the air on the other side of the ball. Davis Alexander threw for 440 yards. Tyson Philpot torched them for 11 receptions and 207 yards, setting two single-season CFL records plus a franchise mark. Rookie first-round pick Rohan Jones hauled in his first career touchdown in the same game.

You don't fix a 440-yard passing day against you by adding a practice-roster receiver. You fix it with secondary help, pass rush, or coverage discipline — none of which shows up on the transaction wire this week. The Janusas move is housekeeping. The Montreal loss was a structural alarm.

What the Transaction Wire Actually Tells You

CFL practice rosters are not developmental academies in the NFL sense. They're emergency depth, ratio filler, and audition space. A player signed in October is not being groomed for 2027 — he's being evaluated for whether he can dress in a playoff game if someone goes down. Janusas's value proposition is narrow and specific: he's Canadian, he's played 11 CFL regular-season games across two franchises, and he's returned kicks. That's a plug-and-play profile for a team that needs a body who won't blow an assignment.

The Bombers have been one of the CFL's most stable organizations over the past half-decade, which makes this kind of churn notable. Stable teams don't usually sign players in October who've been cut twice in the same season. When they do, it's because the depth behind the starters has thinned — through injury, through ratio pressure, or through the simple attrition of a long season.

The Ratio Tax

Canadian receivers carry a structural burden in this league. The ratio demands seven national starters, and receiver is one of the few positions where a team can park a national without gutting its explosiveness. That's why so many Canadian pass-catchers bounce between rosters: they're valuable as ratio solutions even when they're not productive as receivers. Janusas fits that mold exactly — a 229-pound target who can block, play special teams, and satisfy the ratio if called upon.

His collegiate production at Guelph suggests there's a real player underneath the journeyman résumé. But the CFL has never been a league that rewards collegiate production. It rewards fit, availability, and cost. Winnipeg is buying all three at a discount.

Playoff Math Raises the Stakes on Saturday

Winnipeg hosts Calgary on Saturday, October 10 at 7:00 p.m. EDT, with the Stampeders arriving off a 37-31 upset of Saskatchewan. The West is a mess: per 3DownNation's Week 18 power rankings, the Lions, Riders, Stampeders, and Bombers could all host playoff games — or miss out entirely. The crossover berth is live for everyone except Edmonton, and B.C. is suddenly nipping at the Elks for first place.

Calgary at 8-8 is no pushover, and the win over Saskatchewan showed the Stampeders can score in bunches. For Winnipeg, the margin between hosting a playoff game and watching one from the couch is razor-thin. That's why a practice-roster signing matters more than it normally would in Week 19.

When you're 7-8 in a four-team knife fight, every roster spot is a bet. Winnipeg is betting that a Canadian receiver with kick-return history and a Guelph MVP résumé can provide more value as ratio flexibility and special-teams depth than whoever he replaced. It's a small bet. But small bets are what you make when you can't afford a big one.

The broadcast is on TSN in Canada and CFL+ internationally, with radio on 770 CHQR in Calgary and 680 CJOB in Winnipeg. By Saturday night, we'll know whether the Bombers' October patch job was enough — or whether the real gaps were never going to be closed by the transaction wire.
