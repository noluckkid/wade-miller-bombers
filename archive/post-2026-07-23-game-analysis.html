Winnipeg’s Red-Zone Rot: Why 18 Field Goals Mask a Fatal Flaw

The Field Goal Paradox: 18 Makes, Zero Excuses

Through six games, Winnipeg kicker Sergio Castillo has booted 18 field goals—second-most in the league behind Ottawa’s Brett Lauther. The difference? Ottawa is winless at 0-5-1. Winnipeg is 4-2. That contradiction is the central tension of this Blue Bombers season. The offence is averaging 24.3 offensive points per game, eighth in the CFL, despite possessing arguably the league’s most talented skill-position group. Running back Brady Oliveira, the 2023 rushing leader, told reporters after Wednesday’s practice: “We’re moving the ball, we’re executing on offence, we’re just not scoring enough touchdowns right now. Way too many field goals.” The film backs him up. On Sunday’s 36-34 overtime win over Ottawa, Winnipeg drove inside the RedBlacks’ 35-yard line on six separate possessions. They scored touchdowns on only two of those drives. Three ended in Castillo field goals. One ended in a turnover on downs. That’s a 33 percent touchdown conversion rate in high-leverage territory—well below the league average of roughly 45 percent. The issue isn’t moving the chains; it’s finishing them.

Scheme Stagnation: Why the Bombers Stall Inside the 30

Buck Pierce’s offence has a clear identity: power run behind a veteran offensive line, play-action off that run, and deep shots to Kenny Lawler and Dalton Schoen. But when the field shrinks, that identity becomes a liability. Defences compress the box, taking away Oliveira’s cutback lanes, and Pierce has shown a tendency to call predictable inside zone runs on first down. Against Ottawa, the Bombers ran on first down from inside the 30-yard line four times. They gained a total of nine yards. That puts quarterback Zach Collaros in obvious passing situations on second-and-long, where opposing defensive coordinators can dial up blitzes. Collaros has been sacked 12 times this season—tied for fourth-most in the league—and many of those pressures come in the red zone, where the pocket collapses faster. The Stampeders, Winnipeg’s Week 8 opponent, will have watched that film closely. Calgary defensive coordinator Brent Monson loves to bring pressure from the boundary corner, and if the Bombers continue to tip their hand with run-heavy first-down calls, expect the Stampeders to load the box and force Collaros to beat them on second-and-long. The counter? More play-action on first down inside the 30, or quick-hitting routes like slants and pick plays that exploit man coverage. Pierce has shown little of either through six games.

The Oliveira Paradox: Volume vs. Efficiency

Brady Oliveira is the heart of this offence, but his usage is becoming a double-edged sword. He has 82 carries through six games—third-most in the CFL—but is averaging just 4.6 yards per carry, down from 5.3 last season. More troubling: his yards before contact have dropped from 2.9 to 2.1, per CFL stats. That means defences are meeting him in the backfield more often, a sign that opposing fronts are keying on the run and that Winnipeg’s offensive line is losing the leverage battle at the point of attack. The Bombers released offensive lineman Micah Mazzccua from the practice roster on July 22, a move that signals they are not satisfied with their depth up front. Left guard is a particular concern: starter Geoff Gray has been inconsistent in pass protection, and the team has not found a reliable backup. Against Calgary, defensive tackle Mike Rose—a 6-foot-3, 290-pound interior disruptor—will likely line up over Gray. If the Bombers cannot win that matchup, Oliveira’s cutback lanes will vanish, and the red-zone struggles will persist. The solution may be to use Oliveira more as a receiver out of the backfield, where he averaged 9.8 yards per catch last season. Pierce has called only 12 targets to running backs through six games—fewest in the league. That is a schematic oversight that Calgary will exploit if not corrected.

Defensive Fragility: The Other Side of 4-2

Winnipeg’s defence has been just as inconsistent as its offence. The Bombers rank sixth in points allowed per game (26.5) and have surrendered 30-plus points in three of six contests. The secondary, once a strength, has been gashed for explosive plays: Ottawa’s Dru Brown threw for 341 yards and two touchdowns in Week 7, including a 75-yard strike to Justin Hardy that exposed a blown coverage by cornerback Demerio Houston. The pass rush has been the unit’s saving grace—Winnipeg is tied for third in the league with 16 sacks—but it has not been enough to mask coverage breakdowns. Defensive coordinator Jordan Younger has relied heavily on man coverage with single-high safety, a scheme that leaves cornerbacks on islands. When the rush does not get home, quarterbacks have found soft spots in the seams.
