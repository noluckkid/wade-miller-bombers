Collaros Returns, But Winnipeg's Roster Churn Exposes a Deeper Rot

October 10, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Collaros Gamble

Zach Collaros will start Saturday against the Calgary Stampeders, ending a two-game absence caused by a head injury. The Blue Bombers made it official on Friday's depth chart, and the veteran spent the week telling anyone who'd listen that he weighed his health and well-being before deciding to keep playing. That reassurance is doing a lot of heavy lifting for a 38-year-old who has now missed six regular-season games in a single year.

The numbers tell a story of a quarterback managing decline rather than defying it. Collaros sits at 5-4 as a starter, completing 70.6 percent of his passes for 2,204 yards, 11 touchdowns and six interceptions. The completion percentage is fine. The touchdown-to-interception ratio is pedestrian. The availability is the real problem. When your franchise passer suits up for roughly two-thirds of the schedule, every week becomes a referendum on the backup plan — and Winnipeg's backup plan is a carousel.

Dru Brown slots in as the No. 2, Bryce Perkins holds the third-string job, and Taylor Elgersma has been punted to the reserve roster. That's four quarterbacks cycling through a depth chart in Week 19. Elgersma's demotion is the quiet tell: the Bombers are carrying developmental bodies they can't afford to dress, and the reserve list is where you park a project when the roster math gets tight. Winnipeg isn't building for the future here. It's surviving the present.

The O-Line Patch Job

Tui Eli will start at centre in place of Tyler Elsbury, who slides to a depth role. This is a benching dressed up as a lineup tweak, and it's deserved. In last week's lopsided loss to the Montreal Alouettes, Elsbury took a holding penalty, delivered a bad snap, and allowed a sack. That's a three-act disaster in a single afternoon from the most important position on the line — the guy who touches the ball on every snap.

Eli is the steady hand. The 30-year-old has played 85 career regular-season CFL games for Winnipeg at centre and guard since the club drafted him in the fourth round in 2019 out of the University of Hawaii. He made one start at centre earlier this season when Elsbury kicked out to right guard to cover for Patrick Neufeld. So this is the second time the Bombers have had to shuffle the interior because the Elsbury experiment keeps springing leaks.

Here's the uncomfortable pattern: Winnipeg's offensive line has become a game of musical chairs where the music never stops. Neufeld's availability forces Elsbury out of position. Elsbury's mistakes force Eli back in. Every fix creates the next problem. For a team that built its identity on winning the trenches, the interior has turned into a liability that a 38-year-old quarterback with a head injury history can least afford to absorb.

The Linebacker Shuffle

Jaylen Smith returns to the starting strong-side linebacker spot after a hip injury kept him out of last week's game. The Hamilton native has 43 total tackles this season — his second in the CFL — and this will be his seventh start of the year. In a corresponding move, Michael Griffin II drops to a depth role behind him.

Griffin, 28, started in Smith's place last week and has 38 defensive tackles on the season. That's a productive veteran getting demoted, which tells you two things. First, the Bombers value Smith's coverage ability at the SAM spot enough to bench a player who was producing. Second, the depth at linebacker is real — but it's being managed reactively, week to week, rather than through any coherent rotation.

Smith's return matters because the strong-side linebacker in Winnipeg's scheme has to do everything: cover slotbacks, set the edge against the run, and blitz on command. A hip injury is exactly the kind of ailment that saps a linebacker's change-of-direction ability, and Smith will be tested immediately against a Calgary offence that will probe him in space. If he's not fully healthy, Griffin's demotion looks less like a reward for Smith and more like a gamble on a body that hasn't proven it's ready.

The Roster Math Nobody Wants to Do

Step back and look at what this single depth chart reveals. Three starters swapped. A quarterback returning from a head injury. A centre benched for a 30-year-old journeyman. A veteran linebacker demoted for a player coming off a hip issue. A developmental quarterback parked on the reserve list. This is not the roster management of a contender fine-tuning for a run. It's the roster management of a team plugging holes faster than they appear.

The Bombers have spent years winning on continuity — the same core, the same system, the same stars. That model works until the stars age and the depth erodes. Collaros at 38 with six missed games is the headline, but the offensive line churn and the linebacker reshuffle are the supporting evidence. Winnipeg is now a team that has to win the transaction wire every week just to field a competent lineup.

Against Calgary, that might be enough. The Stampeders aren't a measuring stick for much this season. But the deeper question lingers: how many more weeks can Winnipeg patch and pray before the seams split for good? The answer is probably fewer than the standings suggest.
