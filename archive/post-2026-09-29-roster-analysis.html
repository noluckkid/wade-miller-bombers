The Bombers' Five-Man Flyer: Winnipeg Bets on Size in a Four-Team Fight

September 29, 2026 · AI-synthesized analysis

The Five-Man Sweep: Reading the Positional Map

Sunday's transaction wire entry out of Winnipeg looked routine: five American players signed to the practice roster — receiver Jerand Bradley, offensive linemen Tunde Fatukasi and Jeremiah Crawford, and defensive backs D.J. Harvey Jr. and Tamon Lynum. No press conference, no cap headline, no starter. That is precisely why it matters. The positional distribution is the tell. One receiver, two offensive linemen, two defensive backs — the three position groups that erode fastest over a CFL season, and the three where a Week 17 roster is most likely to be held together with tape.

Practice-roster signings in this league are reversible, cheap, and immediate. They are also the only lever a team in Winnipeg's position can pull in late September without mortgaging future cap space. The Bombers are not shopping at the top of the market. They are buying lottery tickets with defined upside — and doing it in bulk, because the hit rate on any single one is low.

Size Over Speed: The Fatukasi-Crawford Line Bet

Start with the offensive line, because that is where the measurements jump off the page. Fatukasi is listed at six-foot-five, 310 pounds, a Far Rockaway, N.Y. product who made 33 starts across 44 career games at Bowling Green State, playing primarily left tackle and guard. Crawford is six-foot-five, 315, out of Goddard, Kan., who played 30 games with 13 starts at tackle at the University of Tennessee and spent time at Butler Community College before attending Carolina Panthers training camp in 2024, where he was released among final roster cuts.

Two six-five tackles with Power Five and NFL-camp exposure is not a random haul. American offensive linemen are the most volatile commodity on a CFL roster: they arrive late, they need a year to absorb the one-yard waggle and the ratio math, and they get poached or injured. Winnipeg has built its recent identity on line play, which makes this a continuity bet as much as a depth bet. If either Fatukasi or Crawford can hold up at tackle, the Bombers buy themselves the ability to survive a November injury without changing their entire protection scheme.

Bradley's Frame and the Contested-Catch Market

Bradley is the most interesting name of the five. At six-foot-five, 241 pounds, he is a specific archetype: the big-bodied, contested-catch target that CFL secondaries — particularly smaller, ratio-driven halfbacks — struggle to match physically. He went unselected in the 2026 NFL Draft, attended Los Angeles Chargers training camp, and made two catches for 34 yards and a touchdown during the preseason.

The college résumé is a trail of production that never quite consolidated: 92 catches for 1,274 yards and 10 touchdowns over three years at Texas Tech, six catches for 94 yards and two scores in one season at Boston College in 2024, then 13 receptions for 184 yards and two touchdowns at Kansas State. That is a player who has been productive in three different systems and never been the focal point in any of them. On a practice roster, that is exactly the profile you want: tools that do not require a playbook rewrite to evaluate.

The Playoff Math and the Wynn Contrast

Context matters here. Week 17 of the 2026 season is in the books, and the playoff field is down to seven teams. Hamilton joined Ottawa as the second club eliminated; Montreal, Edmonton, and Toronto have clinched. That leaves B.C., Saskatchewan, Winnipeg, and Calgary fighting for three remaining chairs. Winnipeg is in the fight — but it is not the team adding at the top of the market. Montreal, the Grey Cup frontrunner, is expected to add veteran defensive tackle Dylan Wynn. That is a contender move: a known quantity, a rotational interior defender, a player who raises the floor immediately.

Winnipeg's five signings are the opposite — a floor-raising exercise conducted at the margins. Harvey Jr. is the other name worth tracking: five-foot-ten, 189 pounds, USC in 2025 with seven tackles and two pass breakups over eight games, after stops at Virginia Tech and San Jose State. Across 45 collegiate games he recorded 101 total tackles, 9.5 tackles for loss, and 14 pass knockdowns. That is a nickel/dime profile, and in a league that increasingly asks defensive backs to tackle in space, 14 pass knockdowns is a number that translates.

The Bombers are not winning the transaction wire this week. They are not trying to. In a four-team race for three spots, the team that survives is usually the one whose practice roster can absorb an October injury without a scheme change. Five signings, three position groups, zero guaranteed dollars. That is not a headline. It is the kind of move that decides whether there is a home playoff game — or a long winter.
