Bombers’ QB Roulette: Elgersma’s Rise Exposes Roster Fault Lines

The Collaros Conundrum: Injury History Meets Cap Reality

Zach Collaros, 36, has started 60 regular-season games for Winnipeg since 2020, but his 2024 campaign is already marred by injury. He missed Week 1 with a hip issue and was pulled in Week 4 against Hamilton after taking a hit to the head. The Bombers have not disclosed the severity, but the CFL’s concussion protocol is now a looming variable. Collaros carries a cap hit of approximately $580,000 in 2024, per league sources — the highest on the team. That allocation leaves little room for a high-priced backup. The result? Winnipeg entered the season with Dru Brown as QB2, a 27-year-old with just 14 career pass attempts. When Brown was also banged up in Week 4, the club turned to Taylor Elgersma, a rookie Canadian from Wilfrid Laurier who had never taken a CFL snap. Elgersma completed 9 of 14 passes for 112 yards and a touchdown in relief, but his inexperience forced offensive coordinator Buck Pierce into a conservative playbook. The Bombers ran the ball 17 times in the second half, leaning on Brady Oliveira to mask the passing game’s limitations. This is not a sustainable model for a team with Grey Cup aspirations.

The Oliveira Workhorse: A One-Back Offense With No Safety Net

Brady Oliveira’s 84-yard performance on 17 carries in Week 4 was vintage, but it also highlighted a troubling roster imbalance. The Bombers have no proven backup running back. Johnny Augustine, the No. 2, has just 12 carries for 48 yards through four games. The team cut veteran running back Greg McCrae in training camp and did not sign a replacement off the transaction wire. When Oliveira needs a breather, the offense stalls. Against Hamilton, Oliveira touched the ball on 6 of the final 8 plays of the game-winning drive — a testament to his value but also a red flag for durability. Oliveira is on pace for 340 carries this season, which would be the highest workload by a CFL back since Andrew Harris in 2019. The Bombers’ offensive line, anchored by left tackle Stanley Bryant (age 38) and right guard Patrick Neufeld (age 35), has allowed 10 sacks through four games — tied for third-most in the league. If Oliveira goes down, the club has no proven option to carry the load. The front office’s failure to add depth at running back during the June free agency window or the July waiver period is a glaring oversight.

The Practice Squad Shuffle: Bargain Bin Band-Aids

Winnipeg’s practice squad, as of July 15, includes six players, none of whom have started a CFL game at quarterback, running back, or receiver. The most notable addition is receiver Kevens Clercius, a 2024 draft pick who has yet to dress for a regular-season game. The Bombers also signed defensive back Jamal Parker on July 10 after he was released by Edmonton, but he has not cracked the active roster. The team’s transaction wire shows no significant moves to address the quarterback depth crisis. The club did not claim any quarterback off waivers after the Week 3 cuts, and they did not sign a veteran passer like Antonio Pipkin or Caleb Evans, both of whom were available in free agency. Instead, the Bombers are betting that Collaros returns quickly and that Brown’s undisclosed injury is minor. That bet is risky. The West Division is tight — Saskatchewan and Edmonton are both 3-1 — and every loss matters. If Collaros misses extended time, the Bombers will be forced to start a rookie Canadian quarterback who has exactly one professional half of football under his belt. The front office’s reluctance to spend on a proven backup is a direct consequence of the cap space eaten by Collaros, Bryant, Neufeld, and defensive end Willie Jefferson — four players over 35 who account for roughly 35% of the team’s salary cap. That is a structural problem that no practice squad signing can fix.

The Injury Report: A Thin Roster Under Siege

Beyond Collaros, the Bombers’ injury report for Week 5 includes starting defensive back Deatrick Nichols (hamstring) and receiver Drew Wolitarsky (knee). Nichols’s absence forces the secondary to rely on rookie cornerback Tyrell Ford, who has been targeted 18 times in three games and allowed 12 completions. Wolitarsky’s injury leaves a receiving corps that already lacks a deep threat; Kenny Lawler leads the team with just 198 yards through four games. The Bombers have not added any veteran receivers off the transaction wire, instead promoting practice squad player Dalton Schoen — who has zero catches in 2024 — to the active roster. The team’s approach to roster management is reactive rather than proactive. They wait for injuries to happen, then scramble to fill holes with unproven talent. That strategy worked in 2021 when the Bombers had a healthy Collaros and a dominant offensive line. But in 2024, with an aging core and a thin depth chart, it is a recipe for a mid-season collapse.
