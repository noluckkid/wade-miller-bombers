Stats Leaders and the Run Defence Crisis

The numbers tell a brutal story. Through three games, the Blue Bombers defence is allowing 128.3 rushing yards per game—third-worst in the CFL and a staggering 22-yard increase over their 2024 average. Opponents are averaging 5.1 yards per carry, and the Bombers have surrendered four rushing touchdowns, tied for most in the league. The culprit is gap discipline; defensive end Willie Jefferson acknowledged after practice that "we've got to be sturdier against the run," a polite way of saying linebackers are over-pursuing and defensive backs are failing to fill alleys. Running back Brady Oliveira is averaging a solid 5.3 yards per carry, but his two fumbles in Week 2 have made ball security a talking point. If the Bombers cannot stop the run, they will force their secondary—already missing injured cornerback Demerio Houston—to defend play-action passes to Tim White and Kiondre Smith. The X-factor is linebacker Jaylen Smith, the Hamilton native who will have over 50 family members in attendance. Smith has 14 tackles and a sack through three games, and his speed in pursuit could be critical in containing Butler. But the Bombers' defensive scheme under coordinator Jordan Younger has been slow to adjust; they allowed 28 points to Edmonton and 24 to Hamilton in Week 1. A loss here would drop Winnipeg to 1-3, tied for last in the West with the Calgary Stampeders, and would give the Tiger-Cats a 2-0 season-series lead—a tiebreaker that could haunt the Bombers in November. The CFL power rankings would almost certainly drop Winnipeg outside the top six, and the narrative would shift from "slow start" to "structural decline."
