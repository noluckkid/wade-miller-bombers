Twenty-Nine Years in the Desert: How the 2019 Bombers Ended the Drought

October 24, 2026 · AI-synthesized from real news sources

On the final weekend of the 2026 regular season, the Winnipeg Blue Bombers did what Winnipeg Blue Bombers have done for a decade: they found a way in. They clinched the CFL's final playoff berth, lost Zach Collaros to injury in the process, and watched Brady Oliveira collect the club's Most Outstanding Player award. The team's own Week 21 game package carried a title that needs no elaboration — "Win Or Go Home."

For anyone who has followed this franchise, that phrase is not a slogan. It is a history lesson. The last time the Bombers dragged a wounded quarterback and an uneven regular season into November, they won three straight road playoff games and ended the longest championship drought in Canadian football. What follows is how that happened — and why it still explains the team you are watching now.

The Long Winter, 1990–2018

Start with the day the lights went out. On Nov. 25, 1990, at BC Place, Mike Riley's Bombers dismantled the Edmonton Eskimos 50–11 in the 78th Grey Cup — still the most lopsided result in Grey Cup history. Linebacker Greg Battle, the CFL's Most Outstanding Defensive Player in both 1990 and 1991, anchored a defence that made the game a formality by halftime. Tom Burgess managed the offence; Robert Mimbs ran behind it. Nobody in the building imagined they were watching the franchise's last championship for a generation.

They were. What followed was a 29-year winter punctuated by five Grey Cup losses, each with its own scar tissue:

1992 — Calgary 24, Winnipeg 10, at SkyDome. 1993 — Edmonton 33, Winnipeg 23, at McMahon Stadium. 2001 — Calgary 27, Winnipeg 19, in Montreal. 2007 — Saskatchewan 23, Winnipeg 19, in Toronto. 2011 — B.C. 34, Winnipeg 23, in Vancouver.

The 2007 game is the one that still stings. Kevin Glenn, the East Division's Most Outstanding Player that season, broke his arm in the East Final; Ryan Dinwiddie started the Grey Cup. Milt Stegall — who retired as the CFL's all-time leader in receiving yards (15,153) and receiving touchdowns (144) — never played in a winning Grey Cup. By 2013 the club had bottomed out at 3–15, and the organization turned itself over to a new president (Wade Miller), a new general manager (Kyle Walters) and, in the winter of 2013–14, a rookie head coach named Mike O'Shea.

O'Shea's first four seasons produced one playoff win. The drought had outlasted four decades of football logic.

Three Road Games, One Trophy

The 2019 Bombers finished 11–7 and third in the West. They lost starting quarterback Matt Nichols to injury in August and handed the offence to Chris Streveler, whose short-yardage packages and running became the league's most awkward matchup problem. Then, on Oct. 9, 2019, Walters sent a conditional draft pick to Saskatchewan for Zach Collaros — a quarterback who had been knocked out of the 2019 season opener by a Simoni Lawrence hit and never taken another snap for the Roughriders.

It looked like insurance. It became the hinge of the entire era.

On Nov. 10, the Bombers went into McMahon Stadium and beat Calgary 35–14 in the West Semi-Final. On Nov. 17, they went into Mosaic Stadium and beat Saskatchewan 20–17 in the West Final. On Nov. 24 — one day short of 29 years to the day since the 1990 triumph — they beat the Hamilton Tiger-Cats 33–12 in the 107th Grey Cup, back at McMahon. Three road games, three wins, no home playoff date.

Andrew Harris, who had spent the season under a two-game suspension and a cloud of scrutiny, ran for 134 yards, added 35 more through the air and scored a touchdown. He was named both Grey Cup Most Valuable Player and Most Valuable Canadian. Willie Jefferson, signed as a free agent from Saskatchewan in February 2019, wrecked the Hamilton protection all night. Winston Rose led the league with nine interceptions. O'Shea's most consequential decision was the least sentimental one: he stayed with Collaros rather than returning to Streveler.

"Win or go home" is not a marketing line in Winnipeg. It is the operating instruction the franchise has carried into every November since 2019 — the season the Bombers stopped waiting for the best team on paper and started being the best team on the day.

Why 2019 Still Matters in 2026

What 2019 unlocked was not a single trophy but a standard. The Bombers have since appeared in five Grey Cups in six seasons — 2019, 2021, 2022, 2023 and 2024, with the 2020 season cancelled by the pandemic — winning in 2019 and again in 2021, 33–25 over Hamilton at Tim Hortons Field. Collaros, the emergency acquisition, became the CFL's Most Outstanding Player in 2021 and 2022. Oliveira grew into a two-time Most Outstanding Canadian and now, per the club's 2026 team awards, its MOP.

Which brings us back to the present. The 2026 Bombers did not arrive at Week 21 looking like a juggernaut. They absorbed a 35–27 loss to Edmonton, then answered with a 34–10 dismantling of B.C. to clinch the final playoff spot — a game they won while losing Collaros to injury. The parallel is not decorative. The 2019 team was not the strongest Bomber roster of the drought era; the 2001 and 2011 teams had more top-end talent and lost. The 2019 team was the one that got its quarterback situation right at the last possible moment, leaned on a veteran defence, and treated the postseason as a separate season.

That is the takeaway for fans heading into this November. Championships in Winnipeg have never been won by the team that looked best in August. They have been won by the team that is still standing, still adapting, and still willing to go on the road. In 1990 it took a 39-point blowout. In 2019 it took three bus rides and a quarterback nobody wanted. The common thread is the same one the club printed on its own game notes this week: win, or go home.
