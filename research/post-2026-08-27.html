From 29 Years of Pain to a Dynasty: How 2019 Changed the Bombers Forever

August 27, 2026 · AI-synthesized from real news sources

On a crisp November evening in 2019, a city exhaled. When the final whistle blew at McMahon Stadium in Calgary, the Winnipeg Blue Bombers had beaten the Hamilton Tiger-Cats 33-12 to capture their first Grey Cup in 29 years — and the dam of collective trauma that had held since 1990 finally broke. Six years later, as the 2026 Bombers prepare for a Week 13 showdown with the Montreal Alouettes, the echoes of that night still reverberate through every practice at IG Field. Zach Collaros is still on the roster, dressed but not active at practice this week. Willie Jefferson is still terrorizing quarterbacks. Mike O'Shea is still on the sideline, now in his 13th season. The dynasty that began that night in Calgary didn't just end a drought — it redefined what Winnipeg football means.

The Weight of 29 Years

To understand what November 24, 2019 meant, you have to understand the decades of heartbreak that preceded it. The Bombers' last championship before that night was a 50-11 demolition of Edmonton on November 25, 1990 — a team led by quarterback Tom Burgess and a defence that forced eight turnovers. It felt like the start of something. Instead, it was the beginning of a 29-year wait that would test the loyalty of even the most devoted fans.

The 1990s and 2000s were a parade of near-misses. In 1992, Calgary beat Winnipeg 24-10 in the Grey Cup. In 2001, the Stampeders did it again, 27-19. In 2007, it was Saskatchewan's turn, a 23-19 gut punch in Toronto. And in 2011, the Bombers — who had started the season 7-1 before a late collapse — lost 34-23 to BC in Vancouver. Each loss carved a new scar into the fan base's psyche. The 2016 West Semi-Final loss to BC (32-31) was another dagger, a one-point heartbreaker on a last-second field goal that felt like the cruelest possible ending to a promising 11-7 season.

By 2013, the franchise was at a low ebb. The team was playing its final seasons at Canad Inns Stadium, the new IG Field project was mired in construction delays and cost overruns — the original $115 million budget ballooned past $200 million — and on-field results were mediocre. The Bombers missed the playoffs in five of six seasons from 2009 to 2014. Something had to change.

The Build: O'Shea, Walters, and a Culture Shift

That change arrived in December 2013, when the Bombers hired Mike O'Shea as head coach. A former CFL linebacker and special teams ace who won three Grey Cups as a player, O'Shea brought a no-nonsense, physical identity. Kyle Walters, who became general manager in 2014, paired with O'Shea to build a roster around a simple philosophy: win in the trenches, develop Canadian talent, and play with relentless discipline.

The early years were uneven — Winnipeg missed the playoffs in 2014 and 2015 before a breakthrough 11-7 season in 2016. But the foundation was being laid. The 2016 signing of Canadian running back Andrew Harris was a statement: the Bombers would invest in elite Canadian talent. The 2018 acquisition of linebacker Adam Bighill and the February 2019 signing of defensive end Willie Jefferson added championship-calibre veterans to a defence that was becoming the team's identity. Stanley Bryant anchored an offensive line that would become the league's gold standard.

The pivotal moment came in July 2019. Starting quarterback Matt Nichols went down with a shoulder injury, and the Bombers made a mid-season trade for Zach Collaros, acquiring the veteran from Toronto for a conditional draft pick on July 31. Collaros had been through injuries and adversity himself — he'd been traded from Saskatchewan to Toronto earlier that year — and his arrival, along with the emergence of Chris Streveler as a change-of-pace weapon, stabilized the offence just in time for a playoff push.

The Breakthrough: November 24, 2019

The 2019 playoff run was a masterclass in resilience. Winnipeg beat Calgary 35-14 in the West Semi-Final, then edged Saskatchewan 20-13 in the West Final — avenging years of playoff pain against division rivals. That set up a Grey Cup date with Hamilton, the league's best regular-season team at 15-3, at McMahon Stadium in Calgary.

It wasn't close. The Bombers' defence suffocated Tiger-Cats quarterback Dane Evans, forcing turnovers and constant pressure, while Andrew Harris — the Winnipeg-born running back who had grown up dreaming of this moment — ran for 134 yards and a touchdown, becoming the first Canadian to be named Grey Cup MVP in over three decades. He also took home Most Outstanding Canadian honours. The 33-12 final score didn't flatter Winnipeg; it undersold their dominance.

"This is for the people of Winnipeg," O'Shea said after the final whistle, a sentiment that captured the emotional weight of a 29-year wait finally ending.

And it wasn't a one-off. The Bombers returned to the Grey Cup in 2021, beating Hamilton 33-25 in overtime at Tim Hortons Field. They lost a heartbreaker to Toronto in 2022 (24-23 at Mosaic Stadium), then beat Montreal 28-24 in 2023. In 2024, they fell to Toronto again, 41-24 at BC Place. Five Grey Cup appearances in six seasons — the 2020 season was cancelled due to COVID — and three championships. That's a dynasty by any measure.

The Dynasty's Legacy — and Today's Connection

As the 2026 Bombers face Montreal in Week 13, the dynasty's fingerprints are everywhere. Collaros, now in his late 30s, remains on the roster — dressed but not active at practice this week, per TSN. Jefferson, at 35, is still a disruptive force off the edge, fresh off another press conference on August 26. O'Shea, now the longest-tenured coach in the league, is still preaching the same gospel of physicality and discipline. The culture they built — resilient, Canadian-first, defence-driven — is the same culture that ended the drought in 2019.

The 2026 team is younger, with new faces at receiver and in the secondary, and the recent loss to Edmonton (a game where the Bombers "wasted a brilliant start," per 3DownNation) shows the dynasty isn't invincible. But the identity hasn't changed. The Bombers still win with defence, still run the football, and still believe they can beat anyone. That belief was forged in the fire of 29 years of disappointment, and it was cemented on that November night in Calgary.

For fans today, the lesson is simple: the drought was never a curse — it was a test. The Bombers passed it, and the dynasty that followed proved that patience, smart management, and a clear identity can overcome even the longest of waits. As the 2026 Bombers chase another title, they carry the weight of that history — not as a burden, but as a foundation.
