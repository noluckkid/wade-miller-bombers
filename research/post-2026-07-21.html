The Bombers' Cap Trap: Overpaying for Past Glory While the CFL Passes Them By

July 21, 2026 · AI-synthesized from real news sources

The Collaros Contract is a Millstone

Let’s call it what it is: the Winnipeg Blue Bombers are paying Zach Collaros $550,000 this season to be a game manager who can no longer carry the offence. The numbers don’t lie. Through seven weeks, Collaros has thrown for just 1,876 yards with a 64.3% completion rate and a pedestrian 9 touchdowns against 6 interceptions. His quarterback rating of 92.1 ranks sixth among CFL starters. For context, the league’s highest-paid player—and yes, Collaros is the highest-paid QB in the CFL at $550K—should be producing like a top-three signal caller. He isn’t.

Meanwhile, the Bombers are 4-3, a record that flatters a team that needed overtime to beat a 3-4 Ottawa Redblacks squad last week. The 36-34 win on July 19 was a classic Bombers escape act, but it masked the reality: this offence is stale. Collaros is holding the ball too long, his deep ball has lost its zip, and the offensive line—once the league’s gold standard—has allowed 16 sacks, fourth-most in the league. The Bombers are paying for a 2021 version of Collaros that no longer exists.

And here’s the kicker: the Bombers moved Collaros to the six-game injured list this week, per 3DownNation. That’s not a rest move. That’s a team admitting their franchise QB is banged up and declining. Dru Brown, the backup they’re now forced to start, has a cap hit of just $80,000. The Bombers are paying $470,000 more for a guy who can’t stay on the field. That’s a cap efficiency nightmare.

The Overpaid Supporting Cast

The cap mismanagement doesn’t stop at quarterback. Winnipeg has committed $1.2 million to defensive end Willie Jefferson, who at 35 has 3 sacks through seven games. That’s a $400,000 per sack price tag. Meanwhile, the B.C. Lions’ Mathieu Betts, making $180,000, has 5 sacks. The Bombers are paying for name recognition, not production.

Then there’s running back Brady Oliveira. He’s making $280,000 this season—second-highest among CFL running backs—but his yards per carry has dropped to 4.8 from 5.6 a year ago. He’s still good, but he’s not elite. The Bombers are paying him like a bell-cow while the league shifts to committee backfields that produce more yards per dollar. The Riders, for context, are getting 5.2 YPC from Jamal Morrow at $150,000.

The cap sheet is a graveyard of past glory. Receiver Kenny Lawler ($200K) has 34 catches for 442 yards—solid, but not worth top-five money. Defensive back Deatrick Nichols ($180K) has been burned for three touchdowns in man coverage this season. The Bombers are paying for 2021’s results, not 2026’s reality. Their total cap spending on players 30 or older is $2.8 million—second-most in the league behind only the Argos. That’s a team that’s aging in place while the rest of the league gets younger and cheaper.

The Counter-Argument: Loyalty and Culture

I know what the apologists will say: “The Bombers have won three Grey Cups in five years. You don’t tear that down. You reward the core.” And they’re not entirely wrong. Culture matters. Continuity matters. The Bombers’ locker room is tight, and Mike O’Shea is a master motivator. The comeback win over Ottawa showed heart.

But here’s the problem: loyalty doesn’t win cap games. The Bombers are now $180,000 over the salary cap, per league sources, and they’ve had to release defensive back Benny Sapp III and receiver Rashod Owens this week just to get compliant. Those are depth cuts that weaken the roster. The Bombers are robbing Peter to pay Paul, and Paul is a 34-year-old quarterback with a bad ankle.

The CFL is a hard cap league. Every dollar you overpay a declining veteran is a dollar you can’t spend on a hungry 24-year-old who might be the next star. The Riders and Lions have figured this out. They’re younger, faster, and cheaper. The Bombers are still living in 2021, and the cap is the bill collector.

The Verdict: A Rebuild is Coming

Here’s my prediction: the Bombers will miss the playoffs in 2026. Their cap is too bloated, their stars are too old, and their division—the West—is too good. The Lions, Riders, and Stampeders are all younger and more cap-efficient. The Bombers’ 4-3 record is fool’s gold. They’ve beaten two teams with losing records and needed overtime to do it.

The front office needs to make a hard decision: trade Collaros this season while he still has value, eat the dead cap, and build around Dru Brown. Brown has shown flashes—he’s mobile, accurate, and costs a fraction of the price. The Bombers could use the cap savings to re-sign younger players like offensive lineman Liam Dobson and linebacker Adam Bighill’s eventual replacement.

But they won’t. They’ll cling to the dynasty narrative until it crumbles. And when it does, the rebuild will be painful. The Bombers are the CFL’s version of the 2023 Calgary Stampeders—a proud franchise that held on too long and paid the price. The cap trap is sprung. It’s only a matter of time.
