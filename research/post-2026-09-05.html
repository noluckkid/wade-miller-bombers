From 29 Years of Pain to a Dynasty: How the 2019 Bombers Broke the Curse

September 05, 2026 · AI-synthesized from real news sources

The Longest Winter: 1990 to 2019

For 29 years, the Winnipeg Blue Bombers carried a weight that no other CFL franchise could fully comprehend. When Tom Burgess handed off to Warren Hudson in the final minutes of the 1990 Grey Cup — a 50-11 demolition of the Edmonton Eskimos at B.C. Place — the celebration in Winnipeg was supposed to be the start of something. Instead, it became the beginning of the longest championship drought in modern CFL history. From 1991 through 2018, the Bombers reached the Grey Cup five times: 1992, 2001, 2007, 2011, and that agonizing 2019 appearance that hadn't yet happened. Each loss carved a deeper scar. The 2001 defeat to Calgary (27-19) saw Dave Dickenson outduel Khari Jones. The 2007 loss to Saskatchewan (23-19) was a one-point heartbreaker decided by a late Andy Fantuz touchdown. And the 2011 collapse against B.C. (34-23) saw Travis Lulay torch a defence that had looked invincible all November.

The drought wasn't just about losing — it was about how Winnipeg lost. The Bombers became the CFL's tragic figure, the team that could reach the summit but never plant the flag. By 2013, when Mike O'Shea took over as head coach, the franchise had cycled through 11 different starting quarterbacks since 1990. The fan base, loyal to a fault, had developed a gallows humour that masked a genuine ache. Every Labour Day Classic, every playoff push, every promising season carried the same unspoken question: Is this the year we finally get it done?

The Collaros Gamble and a Championship Blueprint

The answer came not through a grand rebuild, but through a series of calculated gambles that began in the summer of 2019. On July 31, 2019, the Bombers acquired quarterback Zach Collaros from the Toronto Argonauts for a conditional draft pick. Collaros had been injured, inconsistent, and was on his third team in two years. It was a move that drew shrugs, not headlines. But O'Shea and general manager Kyle Walters saw something else: a veteran with a quick release, elite pocket awareness, and a chip on his shoulder the size of Manitoba.

The other piece was already in place. Andrew Harris, the Winnipeg-born running back who had returned home in 2016, was in the prime of his career. The defence, anchored by Willie Jefferson and Adam Bighill, was the league's most ferocious unit. And the offensive line — featuring Stanley Bryant, the best tackle in the CFL — gave Collaros time to operate. When the Bombers rolled into Calgary for the 107th Grey Cup on November 24, 2019, they were a complete team. The 33-12 victory over the Hamilton Tiger-Cats wasn't just a win; it was a statement. Harris became the first player in Grey Cup history to rush for 100 yards (134) and receive for 100 yards (102) in the same game. Collaros threw two touchdowns. The defence held Hamilton to just 12 points. The drought was over.

"It's not about me. It's about this team, this city, and everyone who waited 29 years. This is for them." — Andrew Harris, Grey Cup MVP, November 24, 2019

A Dynasty Forged in Calgary

What followed proved that 2019 wasn't a fluke — it was a blueprint. The Bombers won the 2021 Grey Cup in a 33-25 overtime thriller against Hamilton, with Collaros throwing the game-winning touchdown to Darvin Adams. In 2023, they beat Montreal 28-24 in a championship that felt like a coronation. The 2022 and 2024 losses to Toronto (24-23 and 41-24) stung, but they didn't break the foundation. O'Shea's culture of accountability, the relentless defensive pressure, and a front office that consistently found diamonds in the rough — this became the modern Bombers identity.

That identity is still visible in 2026. The Bombers' 44-28 dismantling of the Montreal Alouettes on August 29, 2026 — a game that saw the offence explode for 44 points — was a reminder that the 2019 formula still works. The current roster may not have Harris or Jefferson, but it carries the same DNA: a mobile quarterback who can extend plays, a punishing run game, and a defence that creates turnovers. The rivalry with Montreal, which has simmered since the 2023 Grey Cup, is as heated as ever. As 3DownNation noted in its Week 14 picks, the Bombers' "old, stale offence" was the subject of criticism — yet they still found a way to put up 44 points. That's the 2019 ethos: win ugly, win late, win anyway.

What the 2026 Bombers Still Owe to 2019

The 2019 championship did more than end a drought — it redefined what success looks like in Winnipeg. Before 2019, a playoff appearance was a moral victory. Now, anything short of a Grey Cup appearance is a disappointment. That standard, set by O'Shea and carried forward by every player who has worn the blue and gold since, is the true legacy of that November night in Calgary. The current Bombers, sitting in the thick of the 2026 playoff race, are the direct descendants of that team. They carry the same swagger, the same belief that no deficit is too large, no opponent too daunting.

For fans today, the lesson is simple: the drought taught Winnipeg that patience and process matter more than quick fixes. The 2019 team wasn't built overnight — it was assembled over years of smart drafting, free-agent signings, and a coaching staff that refused to panic. When the Bombers take the field this Labour Day, or when they host a playoff game in November, remember that every snap is a tribute to the 29-year wait that preceded it. The pain made the triumph sweeter, and the triumph made the dynasty possible. That's the story of the Winnipeg Blue Bombers — a story that's still being written.
