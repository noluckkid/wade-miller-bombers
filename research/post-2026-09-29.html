How Winnipeg's 29-Year Grey Cup Wait Built a Dynasty—and What Comes Next

September 29, 2026 · AI-synthesized from real news sources

Zach Collaros missed practice with a head injury. Dru Brown got the start against Toronto. The Blue Bombers lost 30-28 to the Argonauts, and by the time they head to Montreal for Week 18 the playoff math is live and the margin for error has evaporated. Playoff scenarios for the 113th Grey Cup are already being mapped across the league.

None of that would land so heavily if the three decades before 2019 hadn't been so barren — and if the six seasons after hadn't been so improbably rich. To understand what's at stake for Winnipeg right now, you have to go back to a night in Vancouver in 1990, and to everything that followed it.

The Longest Winter: 1990 to 2019

On November 25, 1990, the Blue Bombers demolished the Edmonton Eskimos 50-11 at BC Place to win the 78th Grey Cup. It was one of the most lopsided championship games ever played, and it was Winnipeg's tenth Grey Cup. Head coach Mike Riley had a veteran quarterback in Tom Burgess, a punishing defence, and a roster that looked like the start of something. It was, instead, the end of something.

What followed was a 29-year drought that became the defining fact of the franchise. The Bombers didn't disappear — they suffered in public, repeatedly, at the worst possible moment. They lost the 1992 Grey Cup to Calgary 24-10. They lost the 1993 Grey Cup to Edmonton 33-23. They lost the 2001 Grey Cup to Calgary 27-19. They lost the 2007 Grey Cup to Saskatchewan 23-19, handing the Roughriders their first championship since 1989 in a game Winnipeg led late. They lost the 2011 Grey Cup to the B.C. Lions 34-23 at BC Place, in a building where they had won it all 21 years earlier.

Five Grey Cup appearances. Five losses. And underneath them, the lean years: a 3-15 season in 2013 that cost general manager Joe Mack his job, a 5-13 mark in 2015, and a fan base that had watched the Bud Grant dynasty of 1958-62 — four championships in five years — recede into something closer to mythology than memory.

The drought wasn't just a gap between trophies. It was a generation of Manitobans who had never seen the Bombers win a Grey Cup in their adult lives.

The Trade That Ended the Drought

The foundation of the fix was laid quietly. Kyle Walters took over as general manager in 2014. Mike O'Shea took over as head coach the same year. Both were given time that CFL franchises rarely grant, and both used it to build through the draft and through Canadian talent. By 2019, the Bombers were 11-7 and dangerous — but they were also running out of quarterbacks. Matt Nichols went down with an injury in August. Chris Streveler, a running threat with a limited passing ceiling, kept the season alive.

Then, on October 9, 2019, Walters traded a draft pick to Saskatchewan for Zach Collaros — a quarterback the Roughriders had effectively shelved after a series of concussions. It looked like a depth move. It became the hinge on which the modern franchise turned.

Collaros started the West Semi-Final in Regina and beat the Roughriders 23-17. He started the West Final in Calgary and beat the Stampeders 35-14. Then, on November 24, 2019, at McMahon Stadium, Winnipeg beat the Hamilton Tiger-Cats 33-12 to win the 107th Grey Cup and end the 29-year wait.

The story of that game belonged to Andrew Harris. The Winnipeg-born running back, who had signed as a free agent in 2016 and led the CFL in rushing in 2017, 2018 and 2019, ran for 134 yards and a touchdown and swept both Grey Cup Most Valuable Player and Most Valuable Canadian honours. A local kid, on the biggest stage, closing out the longest drought in club history.

Five Straight Grey Cups and the Bill That Came Due

The 2020 season was cancelled by the pandemic, which makes what happened next even stranger. From 2019 through 2024, the Blue Bombers appeared in five consecutive Grey Cup games — a run of sustained excellence unmatched in the modern CFL.

In 2021 they went 11-3 and beat Hamilton 33-25 in overtime at Tim Hortons Field, with Collaros named Grey Cup MVP and later the league's Most Outstanding Player. In 2022 they went 15-3 — a franchise record for wins — and Collaros won a second straight MOP. They lost the 109th Grey Cup to Toronto 24-23 on a late Argos touchdown. They lost the 2023 Grey Cup to Montreal 28-24 when Cody Fajardo found Tyson Philpot in the end zone with 13 seconds left. They lost the 2024 Grey Cup to Toronto 41-24 at BC Place, with Nick Arbuckle — a backup thrust into the job — named MVP.

Two championships and three straight defeats, all by margins that fit inside a single possession except the last. That is the strange arithmetic of a dynasty: the same core that ended a 29-year drought also produced the most agonizing stretch of near-misses in franchise history.

Which brings us to now. Collaros is 37 and coming off a head injury. Dru Brown is taking snaps. The Bombers lost 30-28 to Toronto and are fighting for their playoff lives in Montreal. The 2019 core — Harris, Collaros, the veteran offensive line — has been dismantled by age and the salary cap, exactly as it had to be.

What fans should take from this history isn't nostalgia. It's calibration. The Bombers spent 29 years proving that a proud franchise can lose its way and stay lost. Then they spent six years proving that patient management, a stable coaching staff and one well-timed trade can reverse almost anything. The current season is not a crisis. It is the ordinary cost of having been great for a long time — and the first chapter of whatever comes next.
