Winnipeg Blue Bombers History

A complete timeline of the most storied franchise in Canadian football — from a 1930 founding to 24 Grey Cup appearances and a modern dynasty.

Founding & Early Years (1930–1949)

The Winnipeg Blue Bombers were founded in 1930 as the Winnipeg Rugby Football Club. The "Blue Bombers" nickname was coined in 1935 by Winnipeg Tribune sportswriter Morris Mott, inspired by the nickname of boxer Joe Louis — the "Brown Bomber." The name stuck and became official.

The club quickly became competitive in the Western Canada Rugby Football Union. Their first Grey Cup victory came in 1935, when they defeated the Hamilton Tigers 18-12 at Winnipeg's Osborne Stadium. They added championships in 1939 and 1941, establishing themselves as a western powerhouse before World War II interrupted play.

The post-war era saw the Bombers rebuild quickly. By the late 1940s they were perennial contenders in the newly formed Western Interprovincial Football Union (WIFU), setting the stage for the golden age that would follow.

Golden Era & the Ken Ploen Dynasty (1950s–1960s)

The CFL was officially formed in 1958, and the Blue Bombers were there from day one — winning their first Grey Cup as a CFL member that very year, defeating the Hamilton Tiger-Cats. They repeated in 1959, becoming back-to-back champions.

Ken Ploen

Quarterback · 1957–1967

The greatest quarterback in Bombers history. Ploen led the team to three Grey Cup victories (1958, 1959, 1961) and was named MVP of the 1961 game. His leadership defined an era of dominance that produced four Grey Cup wins in five seasons (1958, 1959, 1961, 1962).

The Bombers' 1961 and 1962 Grey Cup wins cemented the 1958–1962 stretch as the most dominant half-decade in franchise history. Winnipeg reached the Grey Cup in seven of eight seasons during this run — a level of sustained excellence unmatched in CFL history.

The Dieter Brock & Milt Stegall Era (1970s–1990s)

After a 22-year championship drought, the Bombers returned to glory in 1984, led by the legendary quarterback Dieter Brock. Known as "The Blonde Bomber," Brock was a two-time CFL MVP and remains one of the most iconic players in franchise history. His arm strength and charisma made him a fan favourite throughout the early 1980s.

The Bombers won again in 1988 and 1990, with the 1990 championship featuring the electrifying Milt Stegall, who would go on to become the CFL's all-time leading receiver with over 1,000 career receptions. Stegall's 13 seasons with the Bombers made him the face of the franchise in the 1990s.

The Dark Years (1990s–2018)

After the 1990 Grey Cup win, the Bombers entered a painful 29-year championship drought. They reached the Grey Cup multiple times (1992, 2001, 2007, 2008, 2009, 2011) but fell short each time — earning the cruel moniker of CFL's "lovable losers" during this period.

Key moments of heartbreak included the 2007 loss to Saskatchewan and the infamous 2009 Grey Cup loss to Montreal in Calgary. The drought became a defining narrative for a generation of fans.

Modern Resurgence (2019–Present)

The drought finally ended in dramatic fashion in 2019, when the Bombers defeated the Hamilton Tiger-Cats 33-12 in the 107th Grey Cup — played, fittingly, in Calgary. Quarterback Zach Collaros and running back Brady Oliveira would become the engines of a new dynasty.

Zach Collaros

Quarterback · 2019–Present

Acquired from Saskatchewan in 2019, Collaros transformed the Bombers' offence and led them to three consecutive Grey Cup victories (2019, 2021, 2022). A two-time CFL MVP and Grey Cup MVP, he is widely regarded as one of the greatest quarterbacks in league history.

Brady Oliveira

Running Back · 2019–Present

The son of CFL legend Jamel Richardson, Oliveira has become the league's most dynamic running back. His combination of speed, power, and breakaway ability has made him the centerpiece of the modern Bombers' offence. Multiple-time all-star and Grey Cup champion.

The 2021 Grey Cup win in Hamilton and the 2022 victory in Regina gave the Bombers three consecutive titles — their first three-peat since the 1958–1962 dynasty. In 2024, they added a 14th championship, further cementing their status as the CFL's premier franchise of the modern era.

Stadium History

The Bombers' home has evolved dramatically over nearly a century:

1930s–1952

Osborne Stadium — The original home, a modest wooden grandstand facility in south Winnipeg where the team played its earliest games.

1953–2000

Winnipeg Stadium — Known for decades as the team's iconic home. Originally built in 1953, it was renovated and renamed multiple times (Winnipeg Stadium, Canad Inns Stadium). Capacity peaked at over 32,000. The Bombers played their championship seasons of 1958–1962, 1984, 1988, and 1990 here.

2001–2012

Canad Inns Stadium → IG Field Construction — After Canad Inns Stadium closed in 2012, the Bombers temporarily played at the University of Manitoba's Investors Group Field (then called Winnipeg Stadium during transition).

2013–Present

IG Field / Princess Auto Stadium — The current state-of-the-art facility opened in 2013 on the same site as the old Winnipeg Stadium. Originally named IG Field (Investors Group), it was renamed Princess Auto Stadium in 2024. Capacity: 33,000+ for football. Features a retractable roof option and modern amenities.

Ownership & the Wade Miller Era

Wade Miller's involvement with the Blue Bombers represents a new chapter in franchise history. Under current ownership, the Bombers have invested heavily in on-field success (back-to-back-to-back Grey Cups), community engagement, and the modernization of the Princess Auto Stadium experience.

The Miller ownership era has been characterized by aggressive roster building, a commitment to keeping championship-calibre talent in Winnipeg, and an emphasis on making the Bombers a year-round cultural institution in Manitoba.
